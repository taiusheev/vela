# Make Vela simpler around family conversations

Vela should feel modern through **clear priorities, readable content and predictable actions**: put the family’s answer first, make Reply obvious, shorten history into useful previews, and keep composition focused on the person’s words. Official Apple, Android and accessibility guidance supports familiar navigation, restrained visual hierarchy, labeled controls and flexible layouts. Existing messaging and journal products supply useful examples of conversation context, delivery states and chronological records. The recommended direction keeps Vela’s four destinations and warm identity while making routine screens more compact and less decorative. It also preserves explicit targeting, consent, encrypted drafts and uncertain-send recovery. These are research-informed design decisions; no Vela task-time improvement or accessibility certification has been measured. The implementation should be judged by completed tasks, truthful states and readable small-screen layouts, with native-device and family-pilot evidence collected separately.

## Today should lead with the answer and a named reply

Apple’s design principles prioritize necessary functions and direct access to content, while its tab-bar guidance treats tabs as stable top-level destinations with short visible labels. Android recommends navigation bars for three to five destinations in compact windows. Together, these support keeping **Today, Exchanges, Sunday and You** rather than adding another navigation layer or a Compose tab. The sources support consistency; they do not prove these particular names are understandable to Vela’s first-time users. Keep the approved names during this change and test comprehension in the pilot. ([Apple design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles), [Apple tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Android navigation bars](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/components/navigation-bar))

Today should show family context, compact named parent states, the current exchange and its next useful action. The question supplies context; the person’s answer deserves the strongest content emphasis. When an answer exists, a filled, named Reply control immediately below it should take priority over preparing tomorrow’s question. Every parent’s relevant exchange must remain represented. A cleaner layout must not silently hide a second parent to achieve a tidier first viewport. Waiting, accepted questions, paused people and connection problems need their actual state and supported next action.

This order is a Vela hypothesis grounded in analogous patterns. Things distinguishes current priorities from scheduled future items, while Apple Messages keeps the recipient and conversation together and makes reply relationships explicit. Neither source establishes Vela’s task frequencies. The practical transfer is temporal clarity: finish today’s human exchange before giving tomorrow’s preparation equal visual weight. ([Things default lists](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/4001304/), [Apple send and reply guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/send-and-reply-to-messages-iph82fb73ba3/26/ios/26))

One coherent exchange panel can contain question context, answer, media, delivery facts and Reply. Typography, spacing and a restrained rule can separate these parts without nesting rounded surfaces. Android describes a card as a container for a coherent item of content; it does not prescribe a card around every helper or metadata line. Empty states should explain the actual situation and expose the next available action. Errors should retain valid stale content with explicit recovery instead of substituting an example family. ([Android cards](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/components/card), [Apple writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing))

## A restrained visual system keeps daily content prominent

A sans-led interface is the recommended art direction: approximately 28–30-unit screen titles, 17-unit body text and 14–16-unit supporting text, with weight and spacing doing most of the hierarchy work. These sizes are implementation starting points, not universal accessibility minima. Apple encourages a small number of typefaces, clear hierarchy and typography that remains useful when enlarged. An expressive serif can remain in selected reflective passages; the evidence does not establish that serif text itself is inaccessible. ([Apple typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography))

Keep the existing teal action accent, neutral canvas, clear ink and restrained amber details. Use fewer tinted panels and reserve illustration for a meaningful empty or reflective moment. Compact spacing should come from removing duplication and excessive decoration, not shrinking interactive targets or ordinary text. Solid navigation and control surfaces are appropriate for the current cross-platform implementation. Apple’s material guidance confines Liquid Glass primarily to functional navigation and controls and discourages placing it throughout content; a handcrafted glass-card aesthetic is unnecessary here. ([Apple materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials))

**Readability must survive the clean visual treatment.** WCAG 2.2 sets 4.5:1 contrast for ordinary text and 3:1 for relevant control graphics, requires meaning beyond color alone, and includes web text resizing and reflow criteria. Apple recommends at least 44 × 44-point button hit regions; Google recommends 48 × 48 dp touch targets. A shared 48-unit control target is a sensible Vela implementation goal, with platform mapping verified separately. Smaller icons can sit inside larger real layout regions without overlapping neighboring actions. ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [Apple buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Google touch targets](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858?hl=en))

Long names, Chinese text and large text sizes should expand containers and change row layout where needed. Apple’s Larger Text criteria call for enlargement through common tasks and testing across accessibility sizes. English and Traditional Chinese need independent visual review, including mixed-script names, punctuation and button wrapping. A screenshot at ordinary size cannot establish these outcomes. ([Apple Larger Text criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/larger-text-evaluation-criteria))

## Composition should make words easy and recovery unmistakable

The Ask screen should keep three decisions understandable together: **who receives this, what is being sent, and when it will arrive**. Start with the named recipient, a compact horizontal group for Words, Photos and Voice, and a persistent label above the message input. The existing common-format grouping remains a product default; usage data has not established a new ranking. Apple recommends persistent field labels where placeholders would disappear and plain action language that explains the next step. ([Apple text fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields), [Apple writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing))

Optional suggestions and unusual formats belong behind labeled disclosure. The selected uncommon format must remain evident, and disclosure must retain state. Progressive disclosure research recommends presenting core choices first, while warning that separating interdependent decisions across too many screens creates navigation overhead. For Vela, this supports a single coherent composer rather than a mandatory wizard. Suggestions must remain deliberate assistance and must not overwrite a restored personal draft automatically. ([NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/))

A reachable footer should communicate the chosen recipient and timing, with scheduling changes available through a clearly named control. A claimed morning must identify the conflict and require explicit selection of an offered alternative. Cleaner presentation must preserve existing parent-bound attachment resets, encrypted draft restoration, validation and the original retry identity. If an ask or reply has an unresolved saved attempt, recovery should have the strongest action emphasis; two visually equivalent Send and Retry buttons make different operations appear interchangeable.

Delivery wording is a behavioral contract. Signal distinguishes sending, service acceptance, recipient-device delivery and reading. Vela must use only facts its backend establishes: successful acceptance must not become a decorative “Delivered” or “Read” message. Feedback should retain actual returned recipient and date, and uncertain sends should explain uncertainty while preserving the saved request. ([Signal message status](https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/360007320751-How-do-I-know-if-my-message-was-delivered-or-read))

## History and settings should shorten the path to useful detail

Exchanges should become a scannable chronological record: date, named people, short question and answer previews, actual media where useful, and an obvious route into detail. Full replies and playback belong inside the exchange. Day One illustrates date-based entry access, but a calendar-first interface would add controls without demonstrated need for Vela’s 30-day record. A compact week overview remains useful on Sunday; individual days and narratives should retain readable text and truthful destinations. ([Day One calendar view](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/tips-and-tutorials/calendar-view-in-day-one/))

Preview truncation is appropriate when the purpose is choosing an item and the complete text is easy to open. It is unsuitable for consent, errors, safety-relevant explanations or critical action meaning. Recognizable context reduces reliance on remembering gestures and previous screens, so retain names, dates and visible labels instead of replacing them with unexplained icons. This is a design inference from usability guidance, not a measured scanning improvement in Vela. ([NN/g recognition and recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/))

You should group family management, personal preferences and account/help into compact settings sections. Names and precise role or connection states lead each relevant row. Signal’s group settings demonstrate explicit roles and separation between pending invitations and accepted members; Vela’s own consent and channel rules remain authoritative. Invitation dispatch must not imply accepted membership or readiness for a first morning. Destructive actions stay separated and retain confirmations naming the affected person or family. ([Signal group management](https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/360050427692-Manage-a-group))

## Conclusion

The strongest implementation opportunity is better allocation of attention: family words receive space, routine controls become concise, and infrequent choices disclose clearly. Preserve the product’s consent and recovery machinery while changing visual hierarchy. That produces a concrete redesign without introducing another navigation model or unsupported delivery promises.

Acceptance should combine source checks, behavior tests and direct visual review, then add iPhone keyboard, VoiceOver, large-text and real-family comprehension evidence. Web keyboard focus, accessible names and status behavior require deliberate checks; native announcements require native testing. Notification privacy and provider receipts need their own evidence. The resulting app can credibly be described as cleaner and research-informed once reviewed; claims of measured usability gains or complete accessibility compliance require additional proof. ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [Apple accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Apple notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications))
