# The Telegram instrument: flows and services design

2026-09-20. The exact behaviour of the phase-0 instrument (spec Appendix A) on the production platform, and the `@vela/services` modules that implement it. Read with `03-code-design.md` (package rules and APIs), `product/05-product-spec-v2.md` (the product), and `plan/materials/pilot/data-map.md` (what is stored, for how long). Every flow names its database effects, the messages it sends, and the events it records; admin flows also name their `admin_access_log` rows.

## 1. Two facts about Telegram that shape the flows

1. **Group privacy mode.** By default a bot in a group receives only commands, replies to its own messages, and messages that mention it. The family's ordinary conversation never reaches Vela, which is the right default. So a family member makes an ask by **replying to the evening prompt** (text, a photo, two photos, or a voice note) or with **`/ask <text>`** for tomorrow and **`/later <text>`** for a whenever ask. Free-text prefixes like `ask:` are not used. Service messages reach every bot whatever its privacy mode (core.telegram.org/bots/features, "Privacy mode"), so Vela sees a group upgrade (§3.3) and someone leaving the group (`left_chat_member`, §3.16) without subscribing to `chat_member`, which would also need the bot to be an administrator.
2. **Reactions need an administrator.** Telegram delivers `message_reaction` updates only when the bot is an administrator of the group. Organisers are told that making Vela an admin lets reactions count; replies always work.

The webhook subscribes to `message`, `callback_query`, `message_reaction`, and `my_chat_member` (`TELEGRAM_ALLOWED_UPDATES`; `left_chat_member` arrives inside `message`). Its secret is 1 to 256 characters of `A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -`, the set Telegram's `setWebhook` accepts.

## 2. Identity

- A Telegram user is linked to at most one member (`channel_links (channel, external_id)` is unique). A person in two families is out of scope for the pilot; the second family's link attempt is refused with a clear message and logged.
- A kept-light member who taps No is deleted with her channel link (§3.2), and so is one who never answers her invite (§3.15). From then Vela holds nothing that recognises her Telegram user: only the consent proof about her member id (§3.15, "Consent proofs").
- In a private chat the conversation id equals the user id.
- The organiser is a member with `role = organiser`, `billing = true`, and a Telegram link; the kept-light member is a member with a Telegram link and, after consent, `light_on = true`. Neither has a `users` row.
- A group is linked to a family through `family_channels`. Anyone who replies in a linked group is lazily created as a member (`role = member`, name from Telegram, the family's language, the kept-light member's time zone).

## 3. Flows

> **Note: while AI is off** (decision X, 2026-09-18; `03-code-design.md` §7 to §10). A Worker whose var `AI_PROVIDER` is `off` calls no AI provider, and nothing leaves to one. That is staging during the dogfooding week, and development by default; production refuses to start with it. Every AI step below then takes the path it takes when the call fails, but AI being off is not a failure: no `ai_calls` row, no failure warning. Speech-to-text (Deepgram) is not affected.
>
> - **§3.6:** no chips; the question goes out without them.
> - **§3.7:** nothing changes. The hello is copy (`arrival.hello`) and the read-back is `summariseReplies`, so the family's replies are still read back to her.
> - **§3.10:** the voice is still transcribed, and that call is logged. Nothing else a model would give is there:
>   - no summary, mood words, mentions, away, flag, or flag reason is stored;
>   - no flag notice or `admin.flag` is sent, and no away period or `away.confirmed` follows;
>   - nothing is translated: a voice answer's transcript is posted to the group untranslated, a written answer stays as the answer post showed it, and her summary is not translated for her.
>
>   For an answer that reaches understanding, `understood_at` is set on that run, so no understanding re-run (§3.15) or `admin.understand_failed` follows. A failed transcription is re-run as before, and an attempt that ends without a transcript at 3 or more still sends `admin.understand_failed`. Switching AI on later does not go back to the answers understood while it was off.
> - **§3.13:** with no summaries, "what does the family see" answers `parent.family_sees_empty`, followed by the lines of the latest sent weekly read when it has any, although her answers reached the group. This needs a product decision. With no away read from her words, the founder sets away periods on the admin page (`set_away`, §3.17).
> - **§3.14:** the draft is the safe default, no lines and a generic suggestion. Its `weekly_reads.prompt_version` is `ai_off`, and `weekly_read_drafted` carries `ai_off: true`. The founder still gets `admin.weekly_read_draft`, and can type the lines on the admin page before Send.
> - **§3.17:** a sent weekly read is not translated for her; she reads the lines as sent.
>
> The admin overview counts an answer read while AI was off as understood. What shows that AI was off is each Worker's `ai_off` log line (once per start), the `prompt_version` `ai_off`, and the event property.

### 3.1 Organiser onboarding (private chat)

Trigger: `/start` without a parameter from a user with no link, or with an active `onboarding_sessions` row.

| Step | Prompt (copy key) | Accepts | Stores in `data` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `name` | `onboarding.welcome` then `onboarding.ask_name` | text, 1–40 chars | `name` |
| `address` | `onboarding.ask_address` | text, 1–60 chars | `address` |
| `language` | `onboarding.ask_language` with buttons English · 繁體中文 | button | `language` |
| `country` | `onboarding.ask_country` with buttons Taiwan · United States · United Kingdom · Canada · Australia · Singapore · Japan · Germany · India · Other | button | `country` |
| `zone` | `onboarding.ask_zone`, only for countries with several zones (buttons) or Other (type an IANA name, validated by `isIanaTimeZone`: `UTC` and `Etc/UTC` are accepted; fixed offsets such as `+08:00` and the signed `Etc/GMT±N` and `GMT±N` names are refused) | button or text | `timeZone` |
| `wake` | `onboarding.ask_wake` with buttons 06:00 to 09:00 by half hours | button or `HH:MM` text | `wakeTime` |
| `nearby` | `onboarding.ask_nearby` (asks for a name and, if the organiser likes, how the contact knows her; tells the organiser to ask the contact themselves first; never asks for a number) with Skip | text "Name" or "Name, relation" (up to two), or Skip | `nearby[]` of `{ name, relation }` |

The nearby step stores names, never a number (2026-09-17, L8). The text is split at its first `,`, `，`, or `、`: the name is what comes before it (1–40 characters) and the relation what comes after (1–40 characters, or null when there is none). A text that holds a phone number (`PHONE_LIKE`: a run of digits, spaces, and `+ - ( ) .` that holds at least six digits) is refused before anything is parsed: the step repeats with `onboarding.nearby_no_number` and nothing of that text is kept in the session. A contact's number reaches the database only with their recorded yes, on the admin page (§3.17, `record_contact_consent` and `add_contact`).

The organiser's language comes from Telegram's `language_code` (`zh*` → `zh-TW`, else `en`) and can differ from the kept-light member's. On completion, in one transaction: create `families` (the country picks the preferred region: TW, JP, SG, AU, IN → `apac`; US, CA → `us`; GB, DE and other European countries → `eu`; when the preferred region is not in `Config.regions`, the region is `apac`. The pilot configures only `apac`, so every pilot family is `apac`), the organiser member and link, the kept-light member (`status = invited`, `light_on = false`, `arrival_time = wake + 30 min`, `primary_surface = telegram`), `nearby_contacts` with `name` and `relation` only (`phone` null, `consented_at` null: the founder records each contact's yes, with their number, or their no on the admin page, §3.17, and only a contact with a yes is listed in a quiet notice, §3.12), and a single-use `invites` row (token from `Random`, 7-day expiry, `for_member_id` = the kept-light member). Delete the session. Send `onboarding.done` with `https://t.me/<bot>?start=<token>`. Event `family_created`.

A session expires after 24 hours; any unexpected input repeats the current step's prompt. Onboarding buttons use `ButtonAction { type: "onboarding" }`.

### 3.2 Consent (private chat, the kept-light member)

Trigger: `/start <token>`.

- Unknown, expired, or used token: a short "this link is no longer valid, ask the person who sent it" message (new copy key `consent.invalid_link`).
- Otherwise: link the Telegram user to the invite's member (refuse if the user is already linked elsewhere), mark the invite accepted, and send `consent.request` in the member's language, with `{organiser}` the inviter's display name (the family name when the inviter is gone) and `{notice}` the privacy notice URL in her language (`Config.privacyNoticeUrls[members.language]`), and Yes and No buttons (`ButtonAction { type: "consent" }`). The text says who runs Vela (Timur Aiusheev) and links the notice, so the message she taps shows what she agrees to (L6). `message_refs` purpose `consent`.

**What a recorded answer holds** (2026-09-17, L5 and L6, ADR-28). Every answer she gives with a button is one `consents` row: `member_id` = her, `subject_ref` = `member:<her id>`, `kind`, `answer` (`yes` or `no`), `text_version` (the copy key and its version: `consent.request@2`, `consent.health_words@1`), `lang` (her language), `channel`, `given_at` (the tap), and `evidence` built by `chatConsentEvidence` (`proofs.ts`): `chat_id` (the conversation the buttons are in), `message_id` (the message that carries them), `params` (the values filled into the text, derived again at the tap from the sources the send used), and `text_sha256` (the SHA-256, lowercase hex, of the text rendered in `lang` from `params`). The text version and the parameters rebuild the exact message; the hash shows the rebuild is the message she saw.

- **Yes**: in one transaction under her row lock: insert the `light` row (`answer yes`, `text_version "consent.request@2"`, `params {organiser, notice}`); set `light_on`, `light_consented_at`, `light_consent_text = "consent.request@2"`, `status = active`, `light_starts_on = tomorrow (her local date)`, `learning_until = learningUntil(today)`; enqueue `consent.accepted` to her; enqueue the health-words question (next item); tell each organiser `organiser.consent_given`. Event `consent_given` (props `kind light`, `text_version`). After the commit, close the buttons, keeping the request's text (rebuilt from the same params as the evidence) with the chosen label added below it, and tick the member; a tap that records nothing only removes the buttons.
- **The health-words question** (L1, ADR-27). In the Yes transaction, one `consent` outbound to her chat: `consent.health_words` in her language, with `{organiser}` as in the request, and the buttons `consent.yes` and `consent.no` (`ButtonAction { type: "health_words" }`). It is keyed by her conversation with suffix `health_words:<member id>`, so it goes out once per member: nothing sends it again after a stop, a start, or either answer, and only a new member (`create_invite`, §3.17) is asked again. `message_refs` purpose `consent`. It is enqueued with `delaySeconds` 10 (`HEALTH_WORDS_QUESTION_DELAY_SECONDS`), so it arrives after `consent.accepted`: Cloudflare Queues promise no order between two jobs sent back to back.
- **Her answer to health words**. The tap counts only from the person linked to the member the payload names, in a private chat, in a family without `deleted_at`, while she has `light_consented_at` set and status `active`, and while she has no `health_words` row of either answer. Any other tap is acknowledged and changes nothing (logged `health_words_button_ignored` with `reason`: `sender`, `status`, or `already_answered`), so her first answer stands and a redelivered tap records nothing twice. In one transaction under her row lock: insert the `health_words` row (`answer yes` or `no`, `text_version "consent.health_words@1"`, `params {organiser}`). Event `consent_given` or `consent_declined` (props `kind health_words`, `text_version`). After the commit, close the buttons, keeping the question's text with the chosen label added below it. Nothing else is sent, to her or to the organisers, and nothing is ticked. Any later change (a Yes after a No, a new Yes after a stop withdrew one, a Yes given on paper) is made by the founder following `infra/runbooks/data-requests.md`, section B; the admin page has no action for it. What the answer changes is in §3.10, and `stop` withdraws a Yes (§3.13).
- **No** to the light (L7, ADR-28): only while she is `invited` without `light_consented_at`. In one transaction under her row lock: insert the decline row (`kind light`, `answer no`, `text_version "consent.request@2"`, evidence as for a Yes); tell each organiser `organiser.consent_declined` (rows addressed to the organisers' own member ids, so deleting her leaves them); event `consent_declined` (props `kind light`, `text_version`, `member_deleted: true`); `forgetConsentSubjects` for her and her nearby contacts, which cuts the evidence of every consent row about them, the decline row and a `privacy_notice` row the founder recorded for her included, to the proof keys and sets `subject_deleted_at` (§3.15, "Consent proofs"); then delete her member row. The delete removes, through the foreign keys, her channel link, her invite, her nearby contacts, the `message_refs` rows about her, and her outbound rows (the consent request); the `consents` rows stay, with `member_id` and `contact_id` set null. After the commit, close the buttons, keeping the request's text (rebuilt in the transaction, before the delete removes the invite that names the organiser) with `consent.no` added below it, then send `consent.declined` straight to the adapter (`sendOutsideGateway`, as `consent.invalid_link` goes to a stranger), because no member is left to address an outbound row to; a failed send is logged (`direct_send_failed`) and not retried, and the answered request still shows her choice. A second tap, or the same tap delivered again, finds no link and is ignored (`consent_button_ignored`). The family can invite her again only through the founder: `create_invite` on the admin page (§3.17), or the runbook.
- Before a tap, only the buttons answer the request: while she is `invited`, anything she writes in the chat, a command included, is ignored: nothing is stored, sent, or posted to the family group (§3.9). The consent script promises that nothing starts until she taps yes. After a No she is a Telegram user Vela does not know: a message from her gets `help.private`, as anyone's does, `/start` without a parameter starts onboarding, and the old invite link gets `consent.invalid_link`; nothing about her is stored. She opens the link at the end of the founder's call (pilot pack README, step 6) and taps a button herself; the organiser may show her where the button is (consent script §6), because the tap is her written consent.

### 3.3 Linking the family group

- `bot_added` by a user linked as an organiser whose family has no active group: insert `family_channels (kind group, linked_text_sha256)`, the SHA-256 of the `group.linked` text rendered for this post, set `families.language` to the organiser's language, post `group.linked` with `{name}` the kept-light member and `{notice}` the privacy notice URL in the family language (`Config.privacyNoticeUrls[families.language]`: the pilot Worker's own `/privacy` or `/privacy/zh-TW` page, `03-code-design.md` §9), and one button, `group.notice_read` (`ButtonAction { type: "notice_read"; familyChannelId }`, the id of the `family_channels` row just inserted). The organiser is a turn holder.
- **The notice button** (2026-09-17, L9): a tap on `group.notice_read` records that the adult who tapped has read the notice. `handleNoticeReadButton` acknowledges the tap first. It counts only when the payload's `family_channels` row belongs to the family this group is linked to (after a migration the row follows the group, and after a re-link the older message's row still belongs to the family) and the family has not ended (`deleted_at`, or its kept-light member `left` or `deceased`, as in §3.5); otherwise nothing changes. The person who tapped is resolved as any group sender is (`resolveGroupSender`: their member, a member who left made active again, or a member created lazily, §2 and §3.16); a Telegram user linked to another family is ignored. In one transaction under that member's row lock: nothing when the member already has a `privacy_notice` row with `answer yes`, `withdrawn_at` null, and `text_version` equal to `Config.privacyNoticeVersion`; otherwise insert `consents (member_id, subject_ref member:<id>, kind privacy_notice, answer yes, text_version = Config.privacyNoticeVersion, lang = families.language, channel, given_at = now, evidence)`, where `Config.privacyNoticeVersion` is the version line of the notices the pilot Worker serves (`privacy-notice.v1`, `03-code-design.md` §9) and `evidence` is `groupNoticeEvidence`: `chat_id` the group's conversation id, `message_id` the tapped message, and `text_sha256` copied from the button's `family_channels.linked_text_sha256`. Nothing is rebuilt at the tap and no `params` are stored: `{name}` is the kept-light member's name, which must not outlive her No or her deletion in another adult's row, and a later No, `create_invite` or notice URL change must not make the hash name a message the group never received. Event `consent_given` (props `kind privacy_notice`, `text_version`, `source: "button"`). The button stays open for the next adult, and nothing is posted to the group, so each adult who taps is recorded once. Asks, replies, and reactions never wait for the tap: the organiser also sends the notice to each adult individually (pilot pack README, step 4), and the founder records a `privacy_notice` row (`record_consent`, §3.17) for each adult who never taps.
- `bot_added` by anyone else, or to a second group: post `group.not_linked` ("Only the family organiser can connect Vela to a group") and do nothing else.
- `bot_removed`: set `unlinked_at`.
- `migrated` (a basic group upgraded to a supergroup, which Telegram does when the bot is made an administrator): update the linked `family_channels.conversation_id` to `migratedToConversationId` and move that conversation's `message_refs` and its queued outbound rows to the new id, in one transaction (`repointFamilyGroup` in `repo.ts`). Without this the family group silently stops working. Telegram posts a notice in both chats, so one move can arrive twice; the second finds the link already moved and changes nothing. A send refused because of the move re-points the group the same way (§3.7).
- `member_left` (someone left the group or was removed): §3.16.

### 3.4 The evening turn prompt (scheduled)

Action `send_turn_prompt(forDate)` from the kept-light member's tick, at 19:00 her local time. It is not due when tomorrow already has an ask: `loadScheduleInput` sets `tomorrow.askScheduled` when a composed exchange is `scheduled_for` tomorrow (a `/ask`), because a prompted holder's ask would only be queued for whenever.

- No linked group: insert the `turns` row with the organiser as holder and `prompted_at`, send nothing (so the schedule does not ask again).
- Otherwise the holder is `nextTurnHolder(active non-kept-light members by join order, previous holder)`. Insert `turns (family, local_day = forDate, recipient, holder) ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`; enqueue `turn_prompt` to the group (`member_id` = holder, `local_date` = holder's today) with `group.turn_prompt`, or `group.turn_prompt_open` when there is no holder. On send the gateway sets `turns.prompted_at` and `prompt_message_id`, and records `message_refs (purpose turn_prompt, member_id = recipient, local_date = forDate)`. Event `turn_prompt_sent`.

### 3.5 Composing an ask (group)

Triggers: a reply to a `turn_prompt` message (recipient and date from `message_refs`), `/ask <text>` (the family's kept-light member, tomorrow), `/later <text>` (whenever).

When the family has `deleted_at` set, or its kept-light member is `left` or `deceased`, an ask is ignored: nothing is stored and nothing is sent, not even a confirmation. The same holds for replies and reactions (§3.11). Spec §19 promises no automated message of any kind after a death, and a reply to an old turn prompt can arrive for 30 days, while its `message_refs` row lives.

| Content | Exchange |
|---|---|
| text | `question`, `text` |
| voice | `voice_note`, the voice as media |
| one photo, with or without caption | `question` with one image, `text` = caption or null |
| two photos in one album | `photo_choice` with both images, `text` = caption; the second album message joins the exchange created by the first (`options.media_group_id`) |
| more than two photos | the first two |

Media from Telegram is recorded immediately as `media (family, uploaded_by = sender, channel telegram, provider_file_id, provider_unique_id, kind, storage_key null, mime and bytes when Telegram gives them, expires_at = now + 30 days)`. Nothing copies the file into Vela's own storage at this point, and where media storage is off (`MEDIA_STORAGE` `off`: development, and staging until 2026-09-26; decision M of 2026-09-20) nothing ever does: the row keeps the file id, with the mime and size Telegram gave, `storage_key` null and no object anywhere, which is all the group post and the transcript need, because Telegram carries the file. `ingestAnswerMedia` is the only step that writes an object, and only with storage on (§3.10); the job `ingest_exchange_media` is reserved and no flow enqueues it (`03-code-design.md` §9). `provider_unique_id` is Telegram's `file_unique_id`, which the adapter sets for a voice, an audio file, the chosen (largest) photo size, and each album photo. It deduplicates media per family: the insert does nothing on the unique index `(family_id, channel, provider_unique_id)` and the existing row is used, so a file re-forwarded inside one family reuses its row, while another family records its own.

Insert the exchange (`when_rule`, `scheduled_for`, `state composed`, `asker_id` = sender). If the date is taken (the one-per-day index), store it as `whenever` and reply `group.ask_queued`; otherwise reply `group.ask_confirmed`. Set `turns.acted_at`. Event `ask_composed`.

### 3.6 Preparing the morning (scheduled)

Action `prepare(forDate)` at 22:00 her local time. In one transaction: a composed exchange scheduled for that date moves to `scheduled`; otherwise `selectAsk` over her whenever asks moves the oldest to that date; otherwise a `hello` exchange is created (asker null). For a `question`, `ai.chips` drafts three chips into `chips` (failure: no chips, the ask still goes out). Event `exchange_prepared`. The same preparation runs on demand if an arrival is due and nothing was prepared (for example after an outage that skipped the 22:00 wake).

### 3.7 Delivering the arrival (scheduled)

Action `deliver_arrival(date, late)`.

1. Ensure the date is prepared.
2. Read-back: the previous exchange's replies with `to_recipient` and no `read_back_at`, summarised by `summariseReplies` in her language; family voice replies are attached as media before the text.
3. `renderArrival` with her address form, the ask (asker name, on-behalf-of, text, chips, vote options, image count), read-back lines, `late`. The text stays within 4000 characters: read-back lines are shortened first, then the ask text, never the greeting, the asker, or the hint.
4. `enqueueOutbound(kind arrival, member, date, conversation = her chat, exchange, ref purpose arrival)` with the ask's images or voice and the read-back voices as media.

On send, the gateway first commits the send result in its own transaction (`status sent`, `sent_at`, `external_id`, `message_refs`), then applies the kind's effects in a second transaction and stamps `outbound.effects_at` (D-B1): the exchange moves to `delivered` (`delivered_at` = the send time, `delivery_late`), `read_back_at` is stamped on the included replies and the previous exchange moves to `read_back`, and her scheduler is woken. Effects are idempotent. A row already `sent` with `effects_at` null gets its effects applied and is never sent again, so a retry after a failed effect cannot produce a second message. `reconcile` finishes such rows once `sent_at` is more than two minutes old (§3.15) and logs each as a warning, `outbound_effects_late` (level `warn`, fields `outboundId` and `kind`); it is a log line, not an event (`EVENT_NAMES` has no such name). Her answer does not wait for either: the message is on her phone, so her tap on its buttons, or a message from her, first finishes her sent arrivals' effects the same way (`finishArrivalEffects`, logging the same warning with `by: "answer"`), and is then answered as if the delivery had been recorded on time (§3.9). Events `arrival_delivered`, `readback_delivered`.

On a permanent failure (or when the first send and three retries at 5, 15, and 30 minutes have all failed, so on the fourth failed attempt), the gateway sets `delivery_failed_at`, sets `channel_links.blocked_at` when Telegram reports a block, tells each organiser `delivery.failed` once, and wakes her scheduler. No quiet event can open for that date. Event `arrival_delivery_failed`.

Before any send, `deliverOutbound` marks a queued row `dropped` without sending it, and records `gateway_dropped` (props `kind`, `reason`, the reason also in `outbound.error`), when the row's family has `deleted_at` set or the family's kept-light member is `left` or `deceased`. A row enqueued before `mark_left` or `mark_deceased` for her, or before `delete_family` (§3.17), or waiting for a retry at 5, 15, or 30 minutes, therefore never goes out afterwards.

The same drop holds her morning back once she has said stop (§3.13): an `arrival` or a `repeat` whose member is `paused` is dropped, reason `member_paused`, whether the tick that enqueued it read her `active` just before her stop committed or the row was waiting for a retry or a re-drive. Everything else to her still goes, the reply to her stop first. Without this, a morning retried after her stop reached her after "Everything is paused", and its effects woke the scheduler her stop had cleared (fixed 26 September 2026; `gateway.test.ts`). The delivery reads her status before it decides, so the drop is decided again in its own transaction: the row, locked `for update`, must still be `queued`, and her row, read `for share`, must still be `paused`; otherwise the delivery goes on from what is stored now. Her start locks her row `for update` before it makes her active, so a start and a drop take turns on it. Without this, a delivery that read her paused just before her start dropped the morning that the start's tick had just found queued and left to it, lost until a later tick; and one that read her paused as another delivery of the row sent it marked the sent row dropped, so `reconcile` never finished its effects and her start sent the morning again (fixed 26 September 2026; `gateway.test.ts`, the contention drill's `pause-drop-race`). A dropped row keeps its key, so the morning her stop dropped is the one her start brings back: when she says start that day and the schedule asks for the morning while its window is open, `insertOutbound` finds the row the key names `dropped` with `member_paused` and queues it again (`queued`, `attempts` 0, `error` null, `queued_at` as for an insert) with the payload the new request holds, the late note and the read-back as they stand then; it is sent once like any row (`gateway.test.ts`, `parent-commands.test.ts`). No row dropped for any other reason is ever queued again, and a repeat dropped for her pause is never asked for again, because its morning went out before her start.

A delivery job can be lost: the queue send failed after the row's insert committed, or the consumer threw until the job went to the dead-letter queue. The idempotency key then stops every flow from enqueueing the message again, so `reconcile` re-drives the row (§3.15). For a queued row, `queued_at` is when its pending delivery is due: the insert sets it, a retry moves it to the end of the retry's delay, and a re-send to a migrated group or a re-drive moves it to that moment. A row still `queued` more than 10 minutes after its `queued_at` counts as lost. `reconcile` claims it by counting the lost delivery as a failed attempt (`attempts` plus one, `queued_at` = now, `error` = `stranded: no delivery ran by its due time`, written only if the row is unchanged, so two reconciliations cannot both drive it), logs the warning `outbound_stranded` (`outboundId`, `kind`, `attempts`), and enqueues a delivery again. When the lost delivery is the row's fourth failed attempt, the row is failed at once without a send (code `stranded`), with the failure effects of any failed send: for an arrival, `delivery_failed_at`, one `delivery.failed` to each organiser, and `arrival_delivery_failed`. A row whose family has ended in the meantime is dropped as above. Should a late job turn up after all, whichever delivery runs first sends and the other finds the row sent.

A send to the family group (a turn prompt, an answer post, a transcript, a confirmation) can fail because the group became a supergroup: Telegram answers 400 with `parameters.migrate_to_chat_id`, which the adapter reports as `ChannelSendError.migratedToConversationId` (code `invalid_request`). The gateway then re-points the family group exactly as the inbound `migrated` event does (§3.3: `family_channels.conversation_id`, that conversation's `message_refs`, and its queued outbound rows, this one included, through `repointFamilyGroup`), sets the row's `queued_at` to now in the same transaction, and re-enqueues the send at once without counting an attempt. If the row already addresses the id the error names, the error is a permanent failure, so a send cannot loop.

### 3.8 The repeat (scheduled)

Action `send_repeat(date)`: the same rendering with `repeat = true` and no read-back, `kind repeat`. On send the gateway sets `repeated_at`. Event `repeat_sent`. A morning delivered before she blocked the bot has no repeat while her link stays blocked (§3.12).

The schedule decides the repeat from state read before it is enqueued, and the send follows seconds later, or minutes later after a retry or a re-drive (§3.7). So `deliverOutbound` checks again before the send and drops the repeat (§3.7) when its morning no longer waits for an answer: reason `answered` when the exchange has `answered_at`, or an answer of hers arrived on the repeat's local date to any exchange (§3.9, `firstAnswersByDate`); reason `resumed` when she has said start (`start_said`) since the morning was delivered, which stands for that morning's ladder as an answer would (§3.13); and `member_paused` while she is paused (§3.7). Without this, a repeat waiting for its retry when she answered reached her as "in case you missed it" after the family had seen her light, and a tap on its buttons posted her answer to the group a second time (fixed 26 September 2026; `gateway.test.ts`). A repeat already on its way to Telegram when she answers still arrives, as a quiet notice does (§3.12); nothing follows it.

### 3.9 Her answer (private chat)

Who reaches this flow: the kept-light member once she has consented (`light_consented_at` set) and while her status is `active` or `paused`. The same gate holds for her commands (§3.13) and her taps on an arrival's buttons; a tap that fails it is acknowledged and otherwise ignored. A message from her in any other state is ignored, whatever it holds: while she is `invited` (before she taps Yes, §3.2) and once she is `left` or `deceased`, nothing is stored, nothing is sent to her, and nothing is posted to the family group. After a No she is no member at all (§3.2): Vela answers her as it answers any stranger (`help.private`) and stores nothing.

Target: her most recent delivered exchange within the last 36 hours that is not archived. If there is none, the message is posted to the group as `group.answer_text` (or her voice) in one `system` outbound keyed by the group conversation with the inbound event id as suffix. No `answers` row is written and nothing lights; event `answer_recorded` carries `unattached: true`. There is no separate copy key for this case.

A tap on an arrival's buttons (or a repeat's) answers the exchange its button names, which must be hers, delivered, not archived, and able to take an answer; otherwise it is acknowledged and ignored. Nothing takes an arrival's buttons away but a tap on them, so yesterday's buttons still answer yesterday's exchange today.

**A morning on her phone before its delivery is recorded.** The send is committed before its effects (D-B1, §3.7), so when the effects transaction fails, the arrival is on her phone while its exchange is still `scheduled` with `delivered_at` null, until the queue's retry (30 seconds) or `reconcile` (up to about 17 minutes) finishes it. Her answer finishes it first: a tap naming an undelivered exchange of hers finishes that exchange's sent arrival, and a message finishes all her sent arrivals whose `effects_at` is null, before the target is chosen (`finishArrivalEffects`, through `deliverOutbound`, which never sends again). The exchange is then delivered at the send time, and the tap or the message answers it as usual. Without this, a tap in that window was acknowledged and dropped with its buttons left open, she believed she had answered, and the ladder went on to the repeat and a quiet notice; and a message on her first morning went out unattached, which does not count. If the effects fail again, the tap or the message fails, and Telegram delivers it again. A tap on a morning never sent finds no sent arrival and is still ignored (fixed 26 September 2026; `answers.test.ts`).

An answer counts for the local date it arrives on, whichever exchange it attaches to. An answer before the arrival counts for that day (spec §19): a message she sends on a local date before that date's arrival is delivered attaches to the previous exchange, by the rule above, and still counts as that date's answer. So does a tap on an older arrival's buttons, which answers that older exchange. `loadScheduleInput` sets each day's `answeredAt` to the earlier of the day's exchange's `answered_at` and her first `answers.received_at` on that local date (`firstAnswersByDate`), and `openQuiet` and `notifyQuiet` check the same rule again under the day's exchange lock (§3.12). The app's lights read the day the same way (`dayAnsweredAt`, API contract §5), so a day the ladder counts answered, and whose closed quiet told the organisers "Everything is lit again", shows her lit at the time of that answer. The arrival still goes out, and no repeat or quiet notice follows it. A message posted unattached has no `answers` row, so it does not count; that happens only when nothing was delivered in the last 36 hours and no arrival of hers is waiting for its effects, such as on her first morning before its arrival.

Commands come first: `parseParentCommand` (§3.13).

The light path, in one transaction and before anything else:

1. Insert the `answers` row (`kind` from the event; `external_id` = `<conversation_id>:<message_id>`, because Telegram message ids are unique only within a chat, and for a button tap the message that carries the buttons; `payload` with chip, pick, vote, or text; media recorded as in §3.5). A duplicate `(channel, external_id)` ends the flow.
2. Move the exchange to `answered` and set `answered_at` on the first answer.
3. Resolve an open quiet event for that exchange (`outcome answered_late`) and enqueue `quiet_resolved` to everyone who was told. Then lock her exchange for the local date the answer arrives on, `for no key update`, once it has been delivered (`lockDeliveredExchangeForLocalDate`), and when it is another exchange (a tap on an older arrival's buttons) resolve its open quiet event the same way: the answer is that date's answer. The quiet ladder takes the same lock before it opens or notifies (§3.12), so it either sees the answer or waits and is closed here. Without this, a tap on yesterday's arrival after today's notice left today's event open for good, the organisers who were told never heard she answered, and a tick that had decided before the tap opened today's event and sent the notice after it (fixed 26 September 2026; `answers.test.ts`, `quiet.test.ts`). The two closes are one answer, and each organiser hears it once: the day's event tells only the readers the answered exchange's event did not (`resolveQuietOnAnswer` returns whom it told), and both close `answered_late`. Without this, after two silent days, with yesterday's event still open because nobody tapped "she's fine", a tap on yesterday's arrival after today's notice sent each organiser told of both "Mom answered at 14:32. Everything is lit again." twice in the same second (fixed 26 September 2026; `answers.test.ts`). A notice still on its way to a reader when the events close is followed by its own event's close as usual (§3.12). The day's exchange is locked after the one answered, and only once delivered: preparing a morning locks her member row before the exchange, which this transaction writes last, and the arrival's effects lock the new morning before the one read back, which this transaction may hold; a morning not delivered has no quiet to close or open.
4. End her open-ended away periods (`to_date` null) whose `from_date` is on or before her local date and whose `created_at` is before her local date began: `ended_at`, event `away_ended`. An open-ended away lasts until her first answer on or after its start, on a later local date than the one it was set on. A message she sends the day she set it, such as her thanks for `away.confirmed_open` minutes later, comes from wherever she is going and does not show she is back; ending the away on it would bring back the next day's repeat and quiet notice while she is away. The same holds for a period the founder set that day (`set_away`, §3.17). An away understood only after her midnight is set on that later date, so it lasts one day longer, never shorter (fixed 26 September 2026; `pipeline.test.ts`, `answers.test.ts`). **Trade-off, recorded at the reviewers' request (26 September 2026):** a same-day "home now" does not end it either. When she writes the day she set the away that her plans changed and she is home, the away stays open until her first answer on a later local date or the founder's `end_away` (§3.17), so her next morning goes out without its repeat and quiet notice, and a silent next morning tells nobody until she answers. This step runs before any model reads her words, and understanding detects only that she will be away, never that she is back, so nothing here can tell "home now" from her thanks for the confirmation; ending the away on any same-day reply would end it on those thanks, the common case, and bring back the repeat and quiet notice on every morning she is away. The founder ends it by hand when the family says she is back: `end_away` ends any period, hers included, and until the admin page runs in production the statements in `infra/runbooks/data-requests.md` §E end it by its id, whichever `source` it has (`runbook.test.ts` runs them).
5. Event `answer_recorded` (`kind`, latency minutes, type).

Then, outside the transaction:

- Buttons: close them on her message with the chosen label; acknowledge the tap.
- One `ack` per day (`ack.thanks`) through the budget.
- Post to the group, as one `answer_post` outbound keyed by the exchange with the answer id as suffix (one exchange can take several answers, and each is posted): `group.answer_light` (or `group.answer_hello` for a hello or "I'm fine") with the time of `received_at`, followed by the content line (`group.answer_chip`, `group.answer_pick`, `group.answer_vote`, `group.answer_text`) read from `answers.payload`, with her voice or photo attached. `message_refs` purpose `answer_post`. A post never written after the light committed is written by `reconcile` (§3.15, step 3) under the same key: the step failed, which it logs (`after_light_failed`) while the webhook still answers 200, or the Worker stopped between the commit and the post, and the platform's redelivery ended at the duplicate (step 1 above). Without this, a database error in the post step, or a connection lost with the commit's acknowledgement, left her answer lit and never shown to the family, and the lit light kept any quiet notice away, so nobody learned of it (fixed 26 September 2026; `answers.test.ts`).
- Voice: enqueue `ingest_answer_media`. Any other kind: enqueue `understand_answer`.
- Tick her schedule.

### 3.10 Understanding (queues)

Both jobs add one to `answers.processing_attempts` when they start for an answer, so a re-run (§3.15) stops after the third attempt. A voice answer whose transcription succeeds spends two attempts on its first run: ingestion, then understanding. A failed transcription ends the run after one, and the re-run goes back to ingestion.

`ingestAnswerMedia(answerId)`: with media storage on, fetch from Telegram, store in R2 at `families/<family>/answers/<answer>.<ext>`, set `media.storage_key`, `mime`, `bytes` (a row already stored, such as a re-forwarded file, is read from storage instead of being fetched again); with media storage off (decision M), fetch from Telegram on every run and store nothing, so the row keeps its provider file id and its `storage_key`, `mime` and `bytes` stay as they were, a row stored before storage was switched off included, whose object nothing here can open any more. Then transcribe with `stt` (her language as the hint); store `transcript`, `transcript_lang`; log `ai_calls`; enqueue `understand_answer`. A transcription failure is logged and ends the attempt without a transcript; the voice is still in the group.

`understandAnswer(answerId)`: `ai.understand` (her words: her text or caption, the transcript, or the button or option she tapped; the ask, her last three summaries, her address form, her language, the family language for the summary, her local date, and `healthWordsConsent`, below) → `summary` (written in the family language), `mood_words`, `mentions`, `away`; `ai.flag` → `flag`, and `flag_reason` stored content-free as `"<category>:<severity>"` with her health-words consent and as `"<severity>"` alone without it (`unspecified` and `concern` when the model names none; null when the answer is not flagged). `understood_at` is set only when `ai.understand` and `ai.flag` both returned ok; a translation failure does not block it. It is written last, after the rows of the flag's notices, the away with its confirmation, and the post of her words to the group are written, so a run that stops on the way (a lost database connection, a Worker stopped mid-run) leaves it null, the queue retries the job, and the keys below let that run write only what is missing. The flag is stored in the transaction that inserts its notices, so a stored flag never lacks them, and a run whose notices cannot be written stores neither. Every message the two jobs write (the flag's notices, the away's confirmation, the transcript post, `admin.understand_failed`) is inserted as a `queued` outbound row, in the transaction of what it is about when there is one, and its delivery job goes to the queue only after the commit: a send the queue refuses is logged (`understand_handover_failed`, with `errorLabel`) and does not fail the run, because the row is kept and `reconcile` re-drives it within 25 minutes (§3.15, step 2). A wake of her scheduler that fails after an away is set is logged (`away_wake_failed`) and does not fail the run: the away's transaction marked her wake due, so `reconcile` ticks her. Without this, a Durable Object reset during that wake, or a refused queue send for a flag notice, left the answer understood with no `flag.notice` to any organiser, no `admin.flag`, and no transcript post, while the away went on holding back her quiet notices, and neither the queue's retry nor `reconcile` went back to it (fixed 26 September 2026; `pipeline.test.ts`). After that fix a refused send inside the flag's or the away's transaction still rolled them back, so while the outbound queue refused sends for longer than the understand queue's retries last (three, a minute apart), every delivery of the job threw and the job ended in the dead-letter queue with its attempts spent: no `flag.notice`, no `admin.flag`, and no away was ever stored, and the mornings she was away brought the repeat and a quiet notice (fixed 26 September 2026; `pipeline.test.ts`). `reconcile` re-runs an answer only while `processing_attempts` is below 3 (§3.15), and every delivery of a job counts one, so it takes up a run that ended without throwing (a failed model call) and a job that never ran, but not a job that threw on every delivery: that one waits in the dead-letter queue, and the founder hears of it from `reconcile`'s note 25 to 48 hours after the answer (§3.15). A run whose two model calls both fail stores nothing and ends its attempt like any other that is not understood; it used to throw, so the founder's note waited for `reconcile`'s safety net. An answer with nothing to read (a photo without a caption) is understood at once. For words written or spoken in her language (text, voice, photo, sticker, other, chip): translation into the family language when it differs (`translations`, one row per answer and language), then the post to the group as a reply to the answer post (`group.answer_transcript` with the transcript, and its translation, for a voice answer; `group.answer_text` with the translation for a written one; an `answer_post` outbound keyed by the exchange with suffix `<answer id>:transcript`).

When her language differs from the family's, the summary is then translated into her language (`ai.translate`, logged in `ai_calls`) and stored as the answer's `translations` row in her language, after the group post, which matters more. That row is the summary "what does the family see" shows her (§3.13); the answer's row in the family language holds her words' translation. A re-run that keeps the summary keeps the row (and translates it when an earlier translation failed). A re-run that rewrites the summary deletes the row in her language in the same transaction as the summary write, then translates the new summary. A failed translation stores nothing. With no row in her language she reads the summary as stored, so a re-run that rewrites a summary and then stops, or cannot translate the new one, leaves her the new summary in the family language, never the translation of the old one.

**Health words** (2026-09-17, L2 and L3, ADR-27). `hasHealthWordsConsent(db, memberId, receivedAt)` (`repo.ts`) is true when she has a `health_words` row with `answer yes`, `given_at` at or before the answer's `received_at`, and `withdrawn_at` null when the job runs: a Yes given after the answer arrived, or withdrawn before it is understood, counts as none. Every attempt reads it again. `UnderstandInput.healthWordsConsent` carries it to `understand.v4`, which for every answer keeps no diagnosis, test result, or medicine name in the summary or the mentions, and without consent leaves health out of the summary and the mentions altogether (`03-code-design.md` §7). `flag.v2` runs on every answer with the same input as before; its quote leaves those names out where the danger shows without them.

- **With her consent**, what the models return is stored as described above, and a flag reaches the organisers with her words (below).
- **Without it**, services remove, before anything is stored, what the prompt should already have left out: `mentions.health` is stored as `[]` and `mood_words` without `unwell` (the one health word in the mood list), and the `ai_calls.output` of the `understand` call holds the same values (`withoutHealthWords`); the `flag` call's stored output keeps `flag` and `severity` and has `category` and `evidenceQuote` null (`flagWithoutWords`), and `flag_reason` is the severity alone. So understanding stores no health mention, category, or quote for her. The flag check itself still runs on every answer, because it is the safety feature. Her own message still reaches the family group as she sent it (§3.9 and the transcript post above), and its words are deleted after 30 days like every answer's (§3.15).

A flag sends each organiser (they are family) `flag.notice` with her words verbatim when she has given health-words consent, and `flag.notice_no_words` (`{name}` only: no words and no category) when she has not; the admin conversation gets `admin.flag` either way, when configured. With her words, the quote is the model's excerpt when it kept one that is exactly hers, and otherwise everything she said: the AI layer drops an excerpt that is not an exact substring of her words but keeps the flag, so a flag whose quote was dropped still reaches every organiser, with her whole message. `admin.flag` carries only `{family}` (the family name) and `{link}` (the family's admin page, `Config.publicBaseUrl` + `/admin/families/<family id>`), never her words: the admin conversation sits outside `admin_access_log` and retention, and opening the page is logged (§3.17). All are `kind flag` (the budget's one exception), keyed by the exchange and the reader's conversation with the answer id as suffix; the key does not depend on her consent, so a re-run after her answer to the question changed sends nothing twice. Event `flag_raised`, recorded in the transaction that inserts the notices, so a re-run that inserts none records nothing: props `severity` and `words` (whether the organisers' notice carried her words), and, only with her words, `category` and `excerpt` (true when the quote is the model's excerpt); never the quote.

A detected `away` inserts `away_periods (source answer, from_date = away.from, to_date = away.until)`, unless an unended period from an answer with the same `from_date` exists, and replies once to her with `away.confirmed` (or `away.confirmed_open` when `until` is null) as a `system` outbound keyed by her conversation with suffix `away:<answer id>`. Services format `{date}` in her language: en "Sunday 21 September"; zh-TW "9月21日（星期日）", with no spaces around it in the sentence. `away.from` is never before her answer's date. Event `away_set`.

Every AI call is logged. Re-runs never duplicate the transcript post, a translation, a flag notice, or the away reply: each is keyed per answer. The job whose attempt fails at three or more sends `admin.understand_failed` at once (§3.15); `reconcile`'s own safety net for the same note waits until 25 hours after the answer, when no attempt can still be running.

### 3.11 Family replies and reactions (group)

- A reply to an `answer_post` message: insert `replies` (`kind` text, voice, or photo; `to_recipient = true`; dedup on `(channel, external_id)`, with `external_id` = `<conversation_id>:<message_id>` as for answers), move the exchange to `replied`. Event `reply_posted`.
- A `message_reaction` on an `answer_post` message: Telegram sends the member's full reaction set; map emoji to `heart` (❤️ ❤ 🥰 😍 👍 🙏), `laugh` (😂 🤣 😄 😆), `hug` (🤗 🫂), and make the member's reaction rows for that exchange equal to the mapped set. Unmapped emoji are ignored.
- Replies and reactions are ignored, as asks are (§3.5), when the family has `deleted_at` set or its kept-light member is `left` or `deceased`.
- Everything else in the group is ignored.

### 3.12 Silence (scheduled and buttons)

- `open_quiet(date, notify)`: insert `quiet_events` for the day's exchange; if `notify`, notify. The schedule decides from state read before the transaction, so `openQuiet` and `notifyQuiet` first lock the day's exchange (`lockExchangeForLocalDate`) and check again: a morning never delivered, or whose delivery failed, opens nothing; nor does an answered one, whether its exchange has `answered_at` or any answer of hers arrived on that local date, to that exchange or another (§3.9, `firstAnswersByDate`). An answer takes the same lock (§3.9, step 3), so one that lands while the tick decides is either seen here or closes what was opened.
- `notify_quiet(date)`: for each active organiser with a Telegram link, enqueue `quiet_notice` (`quiet.notice` with `{usual}` = the median wall-clock answer time of her last 14 answered days, or `quiet.notice_no_usual` when there are fewer answered days than `TUNING.minSamples` (14); times in her local time; `quiet.nearby` listing, as plain text, the names and numbers of her nearby contacts with `consented_at` set and `declined_at` null, and no `quiet.nearby` line when there are none; buttons `quiet_fine` and `quiet_wait`). The idempotency key includes the notify count, so a re-notification after "wait" is a new message. The gateway updates `last_notified_at`, `notify_count`, and `notified_member_ids`. Event `quiet_notice_sent`.
- `quiet_fine`: resolve with `outcome fine_known` and `resolved_by`, close the buttons, tell the other notified organisers `quiet.resolved_fine`. Event `quiet_notice_resolved`.
- `quiet_wait`: set `wait_until = now + 120 min`, close the buttons with `quiet.waiting`, tick her schedule.
- **A notice that outlives its event.** "Everyone who was told" is `notified_member_ids`, and an organiser joins it only when their `quiet_notice` has actually been sent (the gateway's effect), so a close in the seconds, or the retries, before a notice goes out would tell nobody while the notice still went: "it's been quiet" after she answered, with nothing after it. So the gateway drops a `quiet_notice` whose event has closed before it is sent (`dropped`, reason `quiet_resolved`), and a notice already on its way when the event closes is followed by the close: its effect, which waits on the close's lock on the event, finds it closed and sends that reader the same `quiet_resolved`. The close and the effect share one key per event and reader, so each reader hears it once (fixed 24 September 2026; `quiet.test.ts`, and `postgres-tests/quiet-notice-flight-race.test.ts` for both orders on real connections).
- **Nobody to tell.** Organisers who can be told are the active ones with a Telegram link that is not blocked. When a notice round finds none — a family made in the app, whose organiser has no Telegram link while notices reach only Telegram, or one whose last organiser was marked left or blocked the bot — the round sends `admin.quiet_nobody_told` (`{name}` her display name, `{family}`, `{link}` the family page; no content) to the admin conversation once per round, and logs `quiet_notice_nobody_told` as an error. And the moment such a family is made — the founder marks its last reachable organiser left (§3.17), or a send finds that organiser's link blocked (§3.4) — `admin.organisers_unreachable` (`{name}` the organiser) goes there at once, while the family can still be reached another way. Both are the founder's decision of 24 September 2026; leaving the group is not one of these cases (§3.16).
- **She blocks the bot** (architecture §14: the light pauses without a quiet notice). Her `blocked` event (§5), or a send to her refused as blocked (§3.7), sets `blocked_at` on her link. A morning delivered at or before that mark has no repeat, no quiet event, and no first quiet notice while the link stays blocked: not at T_quiet, and not at the end of the learning period's eight hours for an event opened silently before the block. Vela knows it cannot reach her, and a repeat could only be refused. A notice a wait asked for still comes when the wait runs out, whether the wait was tapped before the block or after it, on a notice or in the app: the organisers already know of this quiet, asked to hear again, and were told when (`quiet.waiting`; spec §8, "the notice returns after 2 h if still quiet"), and she is still quiet. The schedule reads the mark as `blockedAt` (`loadScheduleInput`) and wakes for none of the others, and `openQuiet` and `notifyQuiet` check it again under the day's exchange lock, because a block can land after the tick decided. A morning delivered after the mark went through, so she had unblocked without Telegram saying so, and it is laddered as usual: a stale mark must never silence her quiet notices for good. A notice enqueued before her block still goes, since it was decided while she could be reached, and only a wait on it brings it back; a repeat enqueued before it is refused by Telegram and changes nothing. Her next morning is still sent; refused, it tells each organiser `delivery.failed` (§3.7). Without this, a block at 10:00 after an 08:00 morning brought the organisers "it's been quiet" at 14:00, although Vela knew it could not reach her (fixed 26 September 2026; `silence-drill.test.ts`, `quiet.test.ts`, `schedule.test.ts`). Her `unblocked` event clears the mark and marks her schedule due at once (`markWakeDue` in its transaction, the scheduler after the commit), as a wait does, because a tick during the block woke for none of that morning's thresholds and stored a later, unrelated wake. Without this, a block from 10:00 to 10:45 over the 10:30 tick left her next wake at 19:00: the quiet did not open at 14:00, the organisers were not told at 16:00, and at 19:00 her repeat, their notice, and the turn prompt went out together (fixed 26 September 2026; `silence-drill.test.ts`, `router.test.ts`). Two parts of architecture §14 wait on the founder. The organisers are not told of the block when it happens, only by the next morning's `delivery.failed`, since no copy says it yet. And a repeat or quiet threshold of a morning delivered before the block that passed while it lasted falls due at the tick her unblock asks for, so a block from 10:00 to 10:45 brings the 10:30 repeat at 10:45, because nothing records when she unblocked (an event name or a column, so a migration).
- Nothing is ever sent to a nearby contact in the instrument; the organiser calls them.

### 3.13 Stop, start, and what the family sees

- `stop`: `status = paused`, clear the scheduler (its alarm and Durable Object storage, §3.15), and in the same transaction set `withdrawn_at` on her `health_words` row with `answer yes` and `withdrawn_at` null (L1): saying stop withdraws her health-words consent with the light, and `start` does not give it back (a new Yes goes through the founder, §3.2), so from the stop her answers are understood without it (§3.10). The `light` row is not withdrawn: a stop is a pause she ends by saying start. Send `parent.stopped`, tell organisers `organiser.stopped`. Event `stop_said` (props `health_words_withdrawn`: whether a row was withdrawn). Her morning or its repeat still queued, or waiting for a retry, is dropped by the gateway rather than sent (§3.7, reason `member_paused`).
- `start` (only when paused by her): `status = active`, `light_starts_on` = the later of itself and today, send `parent.started` with the next arrival time, tick. Event `start_said`, recorded in the same transaction as the status; it is the only record of when she resumed, and `loadScheduleInput` reads her latest one since her yesterday began as `resumedAt`. A morning delivered before her start has no repeat and no quiet, at the start or later, and a quiet opened before it is not notified again when a wait runs out. The stop cleared her scheduler, so nothing of that morning's ladder was judged while she was paused, and `light_starts_on` holds back only an earlier day. Without this, after an 08:00 morning, a stop at 09:00 and a start at 19:30, she would get "in case you missed it" right after `parent.started`, and the organisers, told at 09:00 that she asked to pause, "it's been quiet" seconds after she wrote to Vela; a start at 13:55 would bring a 14:00 quiet notice counting the paused hours as silence. Her start is her own message to Vela, so for that morning's ladder it stands as an answer would (§3.9), without lighting the light. The next repeat and quiet belong to the next morning delivered, which is today's when it had not gone out before her start: that one goes out at the start while its window is open (§3.7), with its ladder as usual (fixed 26 September 2026; `schedule.test.ts`, `parent-commands.test.ts`, `tick.test.ts`). That holds for a morning her stop dropped on its way too: its row is queued again (§3.7). A repeat of a morning delivered before her start that was still queued or waiting for a retry at the start is dropped, reason `resumed` (§3.8). **Trade-off, recorded at the reviewers' request (26 September 2026):** a start after a stop settles the ladder of a morning delivered before the stop. After an 08:00 morning, a stop at 09:00 and a start at 09:30, that morning has no repeat and no quiet notice for the rest of its day even if she says nothing after the start, and her light shows `resting`, not `lit`, because a start is not an answer (API contract §5). The organisers were told she asked to pause (`organiser.stopped`) and are not told she started, so a silent afternoon after such a start tells nobody before the next morning's ladder, which runs as usual. The alternative, laddering that morning from the start, would count the paused hours as silence, or send "in case you missed it" right after `parent.started`; her start is a message she wrote to Vela, which shows she could write at that moment as an answer would.
- `what_family_sees` (the keywords in `PARENT_COMMAND_KEYWORDS`, including 家人看到什麼 and 家人看得到什麼 with their 甚麼 and simplified forms): `parent.family_sees_heading` and the summaries of her last seven answered days, dated, in her language, or `parent.family_sees_empty` in their place when there are none; then, when a weekly read has been sent, the lines of the most recent one under `parent.family_sees_weekly_read`, in her language: its `translations` row (`object_type weekly_read`, her language, split at its line breaks), written when the read was sent (§3.17), or `weekly_reads.sent_lines` as sent when the read was written in her language or no translation exists (the translation failed, or retention deleted it after 30 days). Her copy is `renderWeeklyRead` with audience `kept_light_member`: the lines alone, never the count lines, the nobody-asked line, or the suggestion (`sent_suggestion`), because she is never shown missed days (spec §8) and the suggestion is an ask meant to reach her as a surprise (spec §13). When the read has no lines, `renderWeeklyRead` returns null and the reply leaves out `parent.family_sees_weekly_read` too. Each day's summary is that of the latest summarised answer to the day's exchange, read from the answer's `translations` row in her language when the family writes in another language (§3.10), or as stored when there is no such row (the languages match, the translation failed, or retention deleted the row after 30 days).

### 3.14 The weekly read draft (scheduled)

Action `draft_weekly_read(weekEnd)`: services count the days of that week on or after `light_starts_on` (a first week counts only the days since the start) and store the counts in `weekly_reads.stats` with the week's other numbers: `counted_days`, `answered_days`, `hello_mornings` (counted mornings nobody in the family asked, so Vela sent the fallback hello), `family_asks` (the counted mornings whose ask came from a person, so `counted_days` less `hello_mornings`; an older whenever ask delivered this week counts), `usual_time`, `drift_min`, `topics[]` (people, places, plans and dates mentioned on two or more counted days; never `mentions.health`), and `voice_len_drift`. `ai.weeklyRead`, in the family language (`families.language`, set from the linking organiser's language, §3.3), is given only the days she answered (date, answer time, asker or null for a hello, ask type, summary, voice length), her usual answer time, the drifts, and the repeated mentions; never the counts or a day without an answer → `weekly_reads` (`lines`, zero to four, and `suggestion`, kept as the model wrote them; the safe default on failure, no lines and a generic suggestion, so the week is still marked done), then a `system` outbound to the admin conversation when configured: `admin.weekly_read_draft` with `{family}` and `{link}` to the family's admin page, never the lines (keyed by the admin conversation with suffix `weekly_read_draft:<weekly read id>`). Event `weekly_read_drafted`. On the admin page the founder sees the count lines as organisers will read them (`renderWeeklyRead` with audience `organiser`) and cannot edit them, edits the lines and the suggestion, and taps Send (`send_weekly_read`, §3.17): Vela stores the lines and the suggestion as sent and sends each active organiser the counts, the lines, and the suggestion. The draft page tells the founder that the kept-light member can read the sent lines, in her language, by asking what the family sees (§3.13), and never the counts or the suggestion.

### 3.15 Operations (cron)

- Every 15 minutes (`RECONCILE_CRON`, `*/15 * * * *`; every 5 minutes until 2026-09-18, decision W3), `reconcile`, in this order:
  1. Sends whose effects never landed are finished before the ticks, because an arrival sent whose exchange never moved to `delivered` would look undelivered to the schedule (D-B1, §3.7): rows with `status sent`, `effects_at` null, and `sent_at` more than two minutes ago go through `deliverOutbound`, which applies the effects and never sends again. Each logs the warning `outbound_effects_late` (`outboundId`, `kind`), a log line and not an event. Their number is the result's `effects`.
  2. Queued rows whose delivery job was lost are re-driven (§3.7): rows still `queued` more than 10 minutes after their `queued_at`, oldest first. A row that throws is logged (`outbound_redrive_failed`, with `errorLabel`) and left for the next sweep.
  3. Answers whose group post was never written are posted (§3.9, `postMissedAnswers`): answers received between 2 minutes and 24 hours ago, in families without `deleted_at`, whose group on the answer's channel was linked at or before `received_at` and is still linked, and that have no outbound row under the post's key, whatever its status. The post is the one the light path writes, built from the stored answer: the light line with the time of `received_at`, the content line from `answers.payload`, and her voice or photo by its `media` row's provider file id when that row came in on the answer's channel. Each logs the warning `answer_post_late` (`answerId`, `familyId`); an answer that throws is logged (`answer_post_late_failed`, with `errorLabel`) and left for the next sweep. A group linked after her answer never had its post to lose, a row already written (sent, failed, or dropped) is not written again, and the light path and this step share the key, so whichever writes first writes the one post.
  4. Active kept-light members of families without `deleted_at` whose `next_wake_at` is null or more than 10 minutes in the past are ticked; each late one logs the warning `scheduler_missed` and records the event `scheduler_missed` (`late_minutes`). A member whose tick throws is logged (`reconcile_tick_failed`, with `errorLabel`) and skipped.
  5. Answers not yet understood are re-run: those with `understood_at` null, `received_at` between 15 minutes and 24 hours ago, and `processing_attempts` below 3 are re-enqueued, a voice answer without a transcript to `ingest_answer_media` and any other to `understand_answer`. A re-run sends nothing twice (§3.10). Every delivery of a job counts an attempt, so a job the queue retried until it went to the dead-letter queue has spent its attempts and is not re-run; the founder's note (below) is what follows it.
  6. The safety net for the founder's note (next item).
  7. The heartbeat is recorded at the end (`deps.heartbeat.ping()`). The pilot Worker implements the port by storing the time in its singleton `ReconcileHeartbeat` Durable Object, never in Postgres (`03-code-design.md` §9, W1), and `GET /healthz` reports how old that time is. A run that throws, or whose deps cannot be built, records nothing, so a stopped cron, a refused configuration, or an unreachable database all show as a stale heartbeat, which the GitHub watchdog outside Cloudflare turns into an email to the founder (ADR-18, update of 2026-09-18). A failed record is logged (`heartbeat_failed`) and does not fail the run.

  The result is `{ ticked, missed, rerun, effects }`.

  **Timing with a 15-minute reconcile** (W3). Neon's Free plan suspends a project's compute once its monthly compute hours are used, and a reconcile every 5 minutes would keep the database awake all month (`infra/README.md`, section 2). The per-member Durable Object alarms stay the primary scheduler, so arrivals, repeats, quiet notices, turn prompts, and weekly reads still happen at their minute; `reconcile` is the safety net, and each of its thresholds still holds at 15 minutes:
  - **Late ticks:** a member whose `next_wake_at` is more than 10 minutes past is ticked at the next run, so a lost alarm is caught 10 to 25 minutes late (was 10 to 15). The late note still appears only past 180 minutes.
  - **Pending effects:** a sent row whose effects did not land is finished once `sent_at` is two minutes old, so within 17 minutes (was 7). A tick in between that sees the arrival as undelivered enqueues nothing new, because the arrival's idempotency key already exists, and `delivered_at` is the send time once the effects land (D-B1, §3.7).
  - **Stranded sends:** a queued row more than 10 minutes past its `queued_at` is re-driven within 25 minutes of when its delivery was due (was 15).
  - **Lost answer posts:** a post not written once the answer is two minutes old is written within 17 minutes of the answer.
  - **Understanding re-run:** the first re-run comes at the first run after the answer is 15 minutes old, so 15 to 30 minutes after it, and a text answer's three attempts all fall within about 45 minutes (was 25; ADR-25, update of 2026-09-18). The 24-hour window is unchanged.
  - **The founder's note:** the safety net's 25-to-48-hour window is unchanged; runs every 15 minutes still visit each answer many times inside it.
  - **Heartbeat:** `/healthz` answers `stale` once the last successful reconcile is more than 35 minutes old, two missed runs plus five minutes.
- After the third failed attempt the founder is told once: when `ingestAnswerMedia` ends without a transcript, or `understandAnswer` ends without setting `understood_at`, and `processing_attempts` is 3 or more, the job enqueues `admin.understand_failed` at once (`{family}` and `{link}` to the family's admin page, no content) as a `system` outbound to the admin conversation keyed with suffix `understand_failed:<answer id>`, so it goes out once however many attempts follow. `reconcile` enqueues the same note under the same key for a job that never got to it (a crash after counting the attempt), when the admin conversation is configured: answers with `understood_at` null and `processing_attempts` at 3 or more, in families without `deleted_at`, received between 25 and 48 hours ago. It waits 25 hours because stored state cannot tell a crashed job from one still running or queued, and attempts start only when the answer arrives and on re-runs within its first day.
- Each tick stores `members.next_wake_at` in one statement, so a wake marked between the tick's reads and its write is never lost. The tick first reads the stored wake, before anything its decision reads. If the stored value at write time differs from what it read (to the millisecond), another flow marked a wake or another tick stored one while the tick ran, and the sooner of it and the tick's decision is kept, as the Durable Object keeps the sooner alarm. A flow stamps its wake with the time it read before its own work, which can be before the tick started, so no clock reading can tell such a wake apart. A stored value equal to what the tick read (the wake that caused the tick, or an earlier decision) is replaced by the decision, and a null decision stores null. The tick arms the scheduler with the value it stored, logs it, and returns it.
- Nightly, `rollupMetrics` writes yesterday's `metrics_daily` row per kept-light member (her local yesterday).
- Nightly, `applyRetention` implements the pilot rules in `plan/materials/pilot/data-map.md` and records event `retention_deleted` with a count per rule:
  - **Cleared at 30 days.** `exchanges.text` and `exchanges.options` 30 days after `delivered_at`; `outbound.payload` 30 days after `sent_at` (after `queued_at` for a row never sent); and 30 days after the row's own time (`created_at`, `received_at`, `at`, or `opened_at`): `chips` and `translations` (deleted, since those rows hold only text), `replies.text`, the text inside `answers.payload`, `answers.transcript`, `mentions`, `mood_words`, `flag_reason`, `suggestions.text`, `ai_calls.output`, and the reply text inside each `quiet_events.ask_to_check` entry. A cleared NOT NULL column takes its empty value (`''`, `'{}'`, `'[]'`).
  - **Deleted at 30 days.** `message_refs` older than 30 days; `invites` 30 days after `expires_at` or `accepted_at`, except an invite meant for a member who is still `invited` without `light_consented_at`, which stays until that member is deleted and goes with her (below); `onboarding_sessions` whose `expires_at` has passed.
  - **Media** whose `expires_at` has passed and `kept` is false: the R2 object and the row are deleted, a `deletions` row holds `deletionHash("media", <media id>)`, the SHA-256 of `media:<media id>` (L4: never of a storage key, a provider file id, or any other value), and the id is removed from `exchanges.media_ids` and `exchanges.options`, which no foreign key covers; the other references are set null by their foreign keys. Every media deletion, including those below, writes a `deletions` row. Where media storage is off (decision M) there is no object: the proof, the removal of the id, and the delete of the row go exactly as written, and a row stored before storage was switched off logs the error `media_object_unreachable` (the media id and the reason, never the key) and its object stays in the bucket until the founder deletes it, which only staging can reach.
  - **Members** 30 days after `left_at`. In the transaction that deletes them, `forgetConsentSubjects` runs first for them and their nearby contacts (below, "Consent proofs"). Rows that only credit them with an act keep the reference set null; rows that exist only because of them (invites they sent or that were meant for them, outbound messages sent on their tap, nearby contacts near them) are deleted with them (`03-code-design.md` §10).
  - **Invited members who never answered** (2026-09-17, L7): a member with `status = invited` and `light_consented_at` null who has at least one invite meant for her (`for_member_id`) and none whose `expires_at` is less than 30 days ago, so 30 days after her last invite expired. In one transaction: `forgetConsentSubjects` for her and her nearby contacts, then delete her; her channel link (if she opened the link), invites, and nearby contacts go with her. Count `invited_members_deleted`. A member who taps No is deleted at once instead (§3.2).
  - **Families** whose `deleted_at` is set, so within 24 hours of the request: the family's media is deleted first as above, so no R2 object outlives its row (with media storage off there is no object, and the rows go the same way); then, in one transaction, `forgetConsentSubjects` for every member and nearby contact of the family, and the delete of the family and everything that cascades from it.
  - **Consent proofs** (2026-09-17, L5, ADR-28). A `consents` row outlives the member or contact it is about, without holding their data. `forgetConsentSubjects(tx, { memberIds, contactIds }, at)` (`proofs.ts`) runs in the transaction of every deletion of a member or a nearby contact, before the delete: on each row whose `subject_ref` names one of them and whose `subject_deleted_at` is null, it cuts `evidence` to the keys in `CONSENT_PROOF_KEYS` (`chat_id`, `message_id`, `text_sha256`, and `recorded_by`, the marker of a row the founder recorded) and sets `subject_deleted_at = at`. The delete then sets `member_id` or `contact_id` null through the foreign keys. What remains is the ids (`id`, `subject_ref`, `chat_id`, `message_id`), `kind`, `answer`, `text_version`, `lang`, `channel`, the times, and the text hash: enough to show what was agreed or declined, and when, and nothing of what the person said or was called. `consents_subject_deleted_check` refuses a row whose `member_id` and `contact_id` are both null while its `subject_deleted_at` is null or its `evidence` holds another key, so a deletion that skips the step, one made by hand in the Neon console included, fails rather than keeping the words.
  - **After 24 months.** `events` (`at`), `metrics_daily` (`day`), `ai_calls` (`at`), and `outbound` (`queued_at`).
  - **After 5 years** (2026-09-17, L5). A `consents` row that no longer permits anything (`answer no`, or `withdrawn_at` set, or `subject_deleted_at` set) once the latest of its `given_at`, `withdrawn_at`, and `subject_deleted_at` is more than 5 years ago (`PROOF_RETENTION_YEARS`, calendar years in UTC); a standing yes whose subject still exists is never deleted by age, because Vela still relies on it. `deletions` rows whose `deleted_at` is more than 5 years ago. Counts `consents_deleted` and `deletions_deleted`.
  - **Kept** while the family uses Vela: `answers.summary`, the `answers.flag` boolean, and away periods' dates. Consent proofs and deletion proofs are kept as above, whatever happens to the family.

  The job runs the rules in this order: families, expired media, members who left, invited members who never answered, the 30-day clearing and deletion, the 24-month deletion, the 5-year deletion. `retention_deleted` carries one count per rule, the new `invited_members_deleted`, `consents_deleted`, and `deletions_deleted` included.
- Nightly, after `applyRetention`, and only where the API is on (`API_V1` `on`: development and staging, never production), `writeSuggestions` writes tomorrow's suggestion ahead (§3.18, from 2026-09-26). Retention and the suggestions each run whatever the other did: a failure is logged as `nightly_job_failed` with the job's name and its error label, and once both have run the first failure fails the cron run (`cron_failed`).
- A kept-light member's Durable Object storage is cleared, with its alarm, when she stops (§3.13), is marked left or deceased (§3.17), or is deleted, and when her family's deletion is requested (§3.17). Only kept-light members have a scheduler, so a member who leaves the group (§3.16) has nothing to clear.

### 3.16 Departures (group)

Trigger: `member_left` from a linked group. `subject` is the person who left; `sender` is whoever acted, the same person when they left by themself. The bot's own departure never arrives as `member_left` (it is `bot_removed`, §3.3). In a group that is not linked, nothing happens.

Find the member of that family linked to `subject.externalUserId` on the channel:

- **No link** (someone who never replied in the group, or another bot): nothing is stored or sent.
- **A member with `role = member` who is not the kept-light member**: in one transaction set `status = left`, `left_at` = the event's time, and `turns_in = false`, so the next turn prompt names the next holder. Event `member_left`. Nothing is sent. Retention deletes the member 30 days later (§3.15).
- **An organiser or the kept-light member**: no state changes, and their private chats keep working (arrivals, quiet notices, consent). Event `member_left_group`, and `admin.member_left_group` (`{name}` the member's display name, `{family}` the family name, no content) to the admin conversation when configured, as a `system` outbound keyed with the inbound event id as suffix. If anything should change, the founder does it on the admin page (§3.17).
- **Already `left`**: nothing changes, so a redelivered update or a second report is harmless.

No `admin_access_log` row: nobody opened anything.

**Rejoining.** Vela reads no join messages (the adapter ignores them, `03-code-design.md` §6), so a member who is added back is noticed when they next act. When a linked member with `role = member` who is not the kept-light member, and whose status is `left`, asks, replies, or reacts in the linked group, `resolveGroupSender` sets `status = active`, `left_at` null, and `turns_in = true` in one transaction and records `member_joined` (props `rejoined: true`); the message is then handled as usual. The same applies to a member the founder marked left (§3.17) who takes part again. A member added back who never acts stays `left`, and retention deletes them 30 days after `left_at`; after that, their next message in the group creates a new member (§2).

`send_weekly_read` takes the edited lines and suggestion, never the counts. It addresses each outbound row to the organiser (`member_id`, their local day), keyed by the kept-light member, the week's last date, and the organiser's conversation, with the text `renderWeeklyRead` renders for audience `organiser`: the family language, her name as the family calls her, the counts from `weekly_reads.stats` (`counted_days`, `answered_days`, `hello_mornings`, `family_asks`), then the sent lines, then the sent suggestion under `weekly_read.suggestion` (left out when it is empty). A read already sent is not sent again, so a repeated POST changes nothing.

`enqueueOutbound` reports a row the budget index refused as a duplicate, like a repeated key, so a second weekly read for the same organiser on the same local day (the founder catching up a missed week) is not inserted. `sendWeeklyRead` therefore writes `sent_lines`, `sent_suggestion`, and `sent_at` only when at least one organiser's row was inserted. When none was, because no active organiser has a Telegram link or each already received a weekly read that local day, the transaction is rolled back: nothing is stored, logged, or sent, and the page says why, so the founder can send the read the next day. A read recorded as sent always reached at least one organiser, and "what does the family see" never shows her a read nobody received.

After the transaction, when the family language differs from her language, `sendWeeklyRead` translates the sent lines into her language (`ai.translate`, logged in `ai_calls`) and stores one `translations` row (`object_type weekly_read`, the read's id, her language, the lines joined by line breaks) for §3.13. The suggestion is not translated: she never sees it. A failed translation stores nothing, and she sees the lines as sent.

`create_invite` (2026-09-17, L7) is how an organiser invites her again after she said No (her member row is gone, §3.2) or after an invite went unanswered. The founder fills in, with the organiser, what onboarding asked (name, greeting, language, country, time zone, wake time), because a No deleted the profile; the new link reaches the organiser in their own chat, as `onboarding.done` did, so the founder never handles a token. The family's region and group stay as they are. When the family still has a never-consented `invited` member, the new member replaces her: her old link stops working (`consent.invalid_link`), and her nearby contacts, with any yes already recorded, move to the new member, because they are near the same person. The page offers the form only for a family without `deleted_at` and with no member whose light is on or was consented to, and it requires typing `invite`.

`end_away` (2026-09-26) ends a period without releasing what it held back. A period covers its dates until it ends, by `end_away` or by her answer (§3.9), and each repeat and quiet threshold of a day it covers is judged at the instant it is reached (spec §8: in away mode repeats and quiet notices do not happen): one reached before `ended_at` is held back for good and never fires at the end, and one reached after it comes as on any day. `loadScheduleInput` reads the periods not ended and those ended since her yesterday began, and `awayOn(date, at)` is true when one covers the date and had not ended by `at`; a period ended earlier ends before any threshold of yesterday or today. Without that, ending an away on the morning after an unanswered day it covered would send her yesterday's "in case you missed it" an hour before today's arrival and tell the organisers "it's been quiet" about a day the family said she was away, and ending it in the evening of such a day would fire all that day's passed thresholds at once. A day is not held back whole: ended at 08:30, after an 08:00 morning that went out while she was away, the away leaves that day's 10:30 repeat and 14:00 quiet (16:00 notice in her learning period) reached with her at home, and they come at their times. An open-ended away is ended by hand exactly when she has not answered, so holding back the whole day would put the first possible quiet notice about a fall that morning a day later (fixed 26 September 2026; `schedule.test.ts`, `tick.test.ts`).

Nearby contacts' numbers (2026-09-17, L8) arrive only here: `record_contact_consent` takes the number with a yes, and `add_contact` takes one only together with a yes. Services refuse a yes without a number and a no with one (`invalid_payload`), and a no clears the stored number at once, so the contact's row never holds a number without the yes it depends on. A quiet notice rendered before the no keeps its copy of the number in `outbound.payload` until retention clears the payload 30 days after it was sent or queued (§3.15), and in the organiser's chat. A contact added without a yes is a name, and the founder removes one who has not said yes 14 days after being added (pilot pack README, step 7).

### 3.18 Tomorrow's suggestion (nightly, 2026-09-26)

Action: the nightly cron, after retention (§3.15), only where the API is on; production writes none (ADR-31). The app shows the suggestion on Today and puts it in Ask with "Use this" (API contract, § "Tomorrow's suggestion"); no Telegram message carries it yet, and the evening prompt (§3.4) is unchanged.

`writeSuggestions` (`suggestions.ts`), in this order:

1. **Who.** Every member `canBeAsked` (`askable.ts`: of the family, active, light on, consented, not left; the rule the app's compose uses) in a family that has not ended (`familyHasEnded`: no `deleted_at`, and no kept-light member `left` or `deceased`), each for her local days 1, 2 and 3 after today, in her time zone (`SUGGESTION_DAYS_AHEAD`).
2. **Read once, for all of them.** The families' suggestions within 42 days either side of the days, and every used one; and their asks, not withdrawn, scheduled from two days before the first day to the last. A day that has a suggestion is `existing`, a day an ask holds is `claimed`, and neither is written. Only for members with a day still to write: each family's first active organiser (by `created_at`, then id), the people, places and plans in her answers received in the last 14 days, and her latest ask from a person created in the last 30 days.
3. **Pick** the bank item, a pure function of what was read (`pickBankItem`; the rules are in the API contract).
4. **Draft**, outside any transaction: `ai.suggest` (`suggest.v1`) with the organiser's language narrowed to `en` or `zh-TW` (the family's language when there is no organiser), the organiser's name (the family's name when there is none), her display name, the item's type, at most 20 mentions of at most 120 characters (never `health` or `dates`), no family dates, and her last ask. A call that throws, or fails with AI on, logs the warning `suggestion_ai_failed` (member, day, error) and leaves the bank item. A draft is kept only when it has a bank type and words that pass `HEALTH_CHECK` in both languages, `SURVEILLANCE_WORDS`, `GENDERED_PRONOUNS` and `FORBIDDEN_ZH_TW`. With AI off, no call is made and nothing is logged.
5. **Write**, in one transaction: her family `for key share`, then her member row `for no key update` (`03-code-design.md` §8, "Lock order"); an `ai_calls` row for any call the model answered, with `input_ref` `{member_id, for_date, bank_id}`, whatever follows; then `inactive` if she can no longer be asked or the family has ended, `claimed` if an ask now holds the day (`exchangeForLocalDate`), and otherwise `insert … on conflict (about_member_id, local_day) do nothing`: `written` when it inserted, `existing` when another writer was first. A bank row has `text` `''`, `lang` null, `source` `{}` and `prompt_version` `bank.v1`; a draft has its `text`, the organiser's language as `lang`, `source` `{ai_source}` and the call's prompt version. `for_member_id` is null.
6. **Count.** The run returns `{written, existing, claimed, inactive, failed}` and logs it as `suggestions_written`. A member-day that throws is logged as the error `suggestion_failed` (member, day, error label), counted as failed, and the others go on.

No event is recorded and nothing is sent. `writeSuggestionFor(deps, memberId, forDate)` runs steps 2 to 5 for one day of one member, and is `inactive` for a member step 1 would leave out.

**Used by an ask.** Composing in the app (`POST /v1/families/:id/exchanges`) may name the suggestion the ask started from (`suggestion_id`). After the exchange is inserted under her member row's lock, `used_at` is set on it only when it is about her, in her family, and unused; `ask_composed` carries `from_suggestion`, true only then. Any other id composes the ask and marks nothing, and a morning already taken (409) marks nothing. A used suggestion is no longer offered, and no other is written for that day. The Telegram asks (§3.5) never name one.

**Retention.** `suggestions.text` is cleared 30 days after the row was written (§3.15); `bank_id`, `type` and `local_day` stay, so a cleared draft reads as its bank item.
