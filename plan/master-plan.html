Mission and the problem

Why this company exists.

Vela keeps a light on for the people we love who live alone or far away: one daily touch from their family, and the family knows the moment it goes quiet.

Super-aged societies are here. Around 50 million people over 65 live alone in the ten richest ageing countries. Behind each of them is a child, across town or across the world, who carries a daily, unspoken worry: "she didn't pick up." And in the same families, a 20-year-old and a 45-year-old, both busy, both wanting more from each other than a group chat that went quiet.

The insight that organises everything: the worry happens because silence is ambiguous. Every product that ever delivered peace of mind did one thing: it made silence mean "nothing is wrong, because if it were, you'd know."

Ten truths that hold in every country

  1. The buyer is not the user. The child pays; the parent tolerates. Design for both or fail on one.
  2. Two jobs, not one. Emergency (2%) and daily reassurance (98%). Amazon bundled them at $20 and cancelled.
  3. Zero-install wins; hardware-first dies. Everything that scaled rode something already in the home.
  4. Silence needs a plan, not a notification. Elders' own objection: "we don't know what we'd do if an alert fires."
  5. Precision is unsolved. 0.3% true positives in Seoul; nobody publishes numbers. We will.
  6. Willingness to pay is $10–35/month, everywhere.
  7. LTV is capped at 2–4 years by the eldest's trajectory; the relationship must outlive one person.
  8. The 70–80 "pre-dependency" segment is unserved and insulted by everything on offer.
  9. Distribution is the moat. Utilities, telcos, post, councils, remittance apps.
  10. Content from family gets opened with joy; pings get ignored. Famileo and Storyworth vs Enrich and Iamfine.

The product

One app for the whole family. Two promises: closer, and calmer.

Closer · free · for everyone

One moment a day, then the app closes

  • Turns. Each member gets a day; a prompt arrives the evening before.
  • Prompts that know the family, drawn from what people actually said.
  • Grandchildren first-class: a 10-second voice note, a drawing, a photo.
  • Translation both ways, keeping her form of address.
  • Story day: one question a week; her answers become a book.
  • Memory: the Thursday appointment, the exam, the neighbour's name.
  • A safe place: no ads, no strangers, no feed, no infinite scroll.
Calmer · Vela Light · paid

A light kept on for whoever the family worries about

  • The light. Her daily answer lights it on the family's home screen.
  • Quiet notice to the organiser after her usual window, never before +4 h, with the nearby contacts one tap away.
  • Away mode: "уезжаю к сестре до воскресенья" pauses everything.
  • Weekly read: how she really is, in neutral words, from her own replies.
  • Memory and reminders, story archive export, and, later, drift with published precision.
  • Nothing to install, wear, or charge. The family acts; Vela never knocks.

Privacy as product

  • Symmetry. A kept-light member sees what the family sees about her, and can say "не надо."
  • Minimum data. Names, forms of address, cities, hours, 30 days of answers, weekly reads, the archive the family chose to keep.
  • Consent everywhere. Lights are opt-in; nearby contacts agree once.
  • No ads, ever. No selling data, ever. Said on the landing page.
  • Honest language. Not an emergency service. "You'll know within hours, and you'll know who to call."

How it's built. The blueprint is architecture/02-technical-architecture-v2.md (every tool checked against 2026 alternatives in architecture/research/); the data model is architecture/schema.sql, the interface architecture/api-contract.md; decisions ADR-1 to ADR-20 in architecture/decisions.md; the sprint plan is plan/build-plan.md.

Principles that shape the code

  • The light lights on the raw answer, before any AI runs. Nothing on the safety path depends on a model.
  • One outbound gateway enforces the notification budget and idempotency; the anti-addiction promise is code, not discipline.
  • Arrivals are unique per member per day in the database, so double sends are impossible even if two crons overlap.
  • Adapters are pure translation layers; the composer and the ladder never know which messenger they're talking to.
  • Every AI output is logged with its prompt version; an eval set runs on every prompt change.

Failure modes, decided

  • Messenger down: retry 30 min, then tell the organiser once; the ladder does not fire from our own outage.
  • Our worker down at the arrival minute: the next tick catches up; late arrivals carry "sorry this is late".
  • AI down: the light still lights; understanding runs when the queue drains.
  • Wrong time zone: confirmed by the first three answer times; a 3-hour mismatch triggers a check.
  • A kept-light member dies: any member marks it; every schedule stops within the hour; no automated message ever again.

Unit economics. No hardware, no staff on the ladder, AI at cents per family per month. The growth loop. A 20-year-old installs Vela for his grandmother, invites his mother and aunt, the mother switches on the light and pays, the aunt tells her own family. Every family is five to eight people; every member is a potential organiser of another family. Famileo grew to 260,000 families with 70% of subscriptions by word of mouth (research/08).

Channel caution. WhatsApp lost 56% of its Russian daily users to the 2026 block, Telegram is throttled since February 2026, and the state messenger MAX (83M reach) stores chats in plaintext and opens its bot platform only to Russian legal entities (research/10). Telegram is the first adapter; the parent surface in our own app is the floor under every market; the voice line moves to phase 2 because half of Taiwan's 70+ are offline. Remittance apps do not serve Russia, so wave 1 is reached through Telegram relocant communities, not payment rails (research/13).

Open questions

Things worth arguing about, and where the answer will come from.

  • Should the eldest know Vela exists, or does everything appear to come from family? Honesty says she knows; the messenger onboarding says "your daughter asked me to say good morning." Phase 0 tells us how it lands.
  • Is the existing family group the whole onboarding? A bot added to an existing Telegram family group needs no link and no explainer. Cheapest install imaginable; test with two families.
  • Grandchildren as the content engine. Famileo says they post more than children. Does that hold with a 20-year-old on Telegram?
  • Free tier or trial only? Contributors spread the product; but a free daily thread may be enough for many families. Conversion at 90 days decides.
  • Where the founder's own family sits. Which pair do we test on first, and what does the founder's parent say when shown the arrival?
  • What we say when it goes wrong. The first time a kept-light member is found late, what did we promise? Written before the first customer.
