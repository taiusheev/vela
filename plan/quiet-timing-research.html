When should Vela check in, and when should it tell the family?

The family should hear about a silent morning before lunch, not in the afternoon. Each step should wait for Mom's own rhythm, so it doesn't cry wolf.

The short answer

Check in 1 hour after her usual answer time. Tell the organisers 90 minutes after that, if she's still silent. In her first 2 weeks, use 2 hours and 4 hours. "Not okay" goes out at once. Today, a silent morning in her first 2 weeks reaches the family only after 8 hours.

What Vela can promise, and what it can't

Vela is a same-morning safety net, not a fall alarm. A fall alarm acts in minutes. Vela can only notice that a morning went quiet. The evidence says that still matters: help after a fall is often slow, and the alarm button usually isn't pressed.

82%of falls among people over 90 happened when they were alone
80%of those falls happened while an alarm was available, but nobody pressed it
15%of falls ended with 1 hour or more on the floor, 10% with more than 2 hours
60%of people who lay 1 hour or more were admitted to hospital

All four numbers come from one study (Fleming and Brayne, BMJ 2008, 110 people over 90). Doctors call 1 hour on the floor a "long lie". A UK research team (NIHR, 2026) says there is still too little evidence to tell which hour makes the difference. So, sooner is better, but no study names a safe deadline.

This sets the honest promise for our website and onboarding: "If Mom's morning goes quiet, you'll know before lunch." If a parent has fallen before, families should also get a fall-detection watch or pendant. Vela doesn't replace that.

What other services do

Two groups. Phone-call services act within an hour, because an unanswered phone means nobody is there. Message and sensor services wait 4 to 48 hours, because people read messages late.

Every service that lasted uses the same ladder: ask, ask once more, then tell a person. Nobody tells the family on the first silence. Vela already has this ladder. The check-in we just built is the "ask once more", and now it can ask a real question: is she okay?

Why not tell the family sooner?

Silence is normal. Our earlier research found that even willing, healthy parents leave about 1 morning in 4 or 5 unanswered. With a notice on every silent morning, a family would get 5 to 7 notices a month. After a few false alarms, people stop reacting. Then the one real notice gets ignored too.

False alarms also change the parent. In a University of Washington study, older adults changed their habits to avoid setting off alerts to their children. Some rushed in the bathroom, which itself risks a fall. A notice should feel rare and worth acting on.

Nobody publishes their false-alarm rate. No service above says how often its alerts turn out to be nothing. So the exact minutes below are our best estimate. We will set them from our own families' numbers after 4 weeks.

Proposed rules

  1. Morning, at her chosen time. No buttons. She answers in her own words. Built (behind a switch).
  2. Check-in: her usual answer time + 1 hour. Never sooner than 1 hour after the morning, never later than 2.5 hours. First 2 weeks: 2 hours. Today it is a fixed 2.5 hours for everyone.
  3. "Not okay" tapped: at once. Every organiser and the founder are messaged. Built. Next step: a phone notification from the Vela app as well.
  4. Quiet notice: 90 minutes after the check-in, with message and phone notification. Never earlier than 1 hour after her late days, and never later than 5 hours after the morning. First 2 weeks: 4 hours, both message and phone. Today the latest is 10 hours, and in her first 2 weeks only the app shows it until 8 hours.
  5. Nobody acts on the notice: tell them again after 2 hours, and tell the founder. Today a notice comes back only if someone taps "Wait 2 hours". If nobody taps anything, nothing more happens.
  6. Still silent at the next morning: the founder phones the organiser. A person checks that the family knows.

These rules leave a few things as they are: away mode, the quiet notice wording, nearby contacts, and the rule that a notice never counts time before her message was delivered.

Let the family choose how careful

Families differ. A parent who fell last month needs a faster notice than one who goes to the market every morning. The organiser picks one setting per parent in the app:

How we'll know it works

We'll turn this on in our test copy first, then measure it for the first 4 weeks with real families:

  1. How long each parent takes to answer. Typical days and late days, per parent.
  2. How many answers come only after the check-in. This shows whether the check-in is too early or too late.
  3. How many notices end with an answer within 1 hour. These are false alarms. Target: notices per parent fewer than 4 a month, as the spec already says.
  4. How fast an organiser acts after a notice. If organisers wait hours, a faster notice doesn't help, and the second notice and founder call matter more.

How sure are we

Sure: a two-step ladder (check in, then tell a person), a notice the same morning instead of the afternoon, "Not okay" at once, and a second notice when nobody acts. Every lasting service and the falls evidence point this way.

Less sure: the exact minutes (1 hour, 90 minutes, 5 hours). No service publishes false-alarm data, and we don't have our own numbers yet. Treat these as starting values, then adjust them after 4 weeks.
