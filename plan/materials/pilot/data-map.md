Version `data-map.v1` · 20 September 2026 · for the founder, the co-founder and counsel · sources: spec §17, architecture §5 and §12, `architecture/schema.sql` with the changes in `architecture/03-code-design.md` §3, `architecture/04-instrument-flows.md` and the sprint 1 contract decisions of 14 September 2026 (`architecture/03-code-design.md` §10; `architecture/decisions.md` ADR-21 to ADR-25), the hosting decision of 17 September 2026 (ADR-26), the founder's decisions of 17 September 2026 (a free pilot, the notes tool, the benchmark bucket's account), the decisions of 17 September 2026 that apply `legal-memo.md` (L1 to L13; `architecture/decisions.md` ADR-27 and ADR-28; flows §3.1, §3.2, §3.3, §3.10, §3.15, §3.17), the decisions of 18 September 2026 (a dogfooding week on staging first, `README.md` decision 19; a Neon project for each environment, `infra/README.md` section 2), the decision of 20 September 2026 (media storage off outside production, decision M; `architecture/03-code-design.md` §10, `infra/README.md` section 1, step 3), `infra/sub-processors.md`

Every category of personal data Vela handles in the pilot: where it comes from, where it lives, why, how long, who can see it, and which sub-processors touch it. The privacy notice (`privacy-notice.en.md`) is the plain-language version of this table; when one changes, change the other in the same commit.

**Retention column.** *Defined* means a source document sets it (quoted). *Pilot rule* means the sources left it open and this pack set it. ADR-24 puts this pack's rules for Vela's database and Durable Object storage into the retention job (`applyRetention` in `@vela/services`, flows §3.15, build plan 2.8), except the 24-month limit on `admin_access_log` (gap 18). Until that job ships with its retention tests, the privacy notice is not used with any real family (the founder's own family is not in the pilot, `README.md`, decision 19). See "Gaps" at the end.

**Who can see.** Family = members of the family's Vela group · Organisers = organisers of that family · The person = the kept-light member · Contact = a nearby contact · Founder = the founder through the admin page (the admin Worker `vela-admin`, behind Cloudflare Access, ADR-26; every view and every admin action logged in `admin_access_log`, flows §3.17) or the Neon console (logged by hand, `infra/runbooks/data-requests.md`); the admin conversation on Telegram carries no family content (row 25, ADR-21) · Co-founder = the AI coding agent, content-free data only (see `infra/README.md`, access rules).

**Sub-processors.** CF = Cloudflare (the Workers `vela` and `vela-admin`, Durable Objects, Queues, R2, Hyperdrive, Access) · Neon · Anthropic · Deepgram · Sentry · TG = Telegram · LINE (from sprint 2) · Google = Google Drive in the founder's own Google account, holding the founder's notes in a private folder "Vela pilot" shared with no one (Google's servers in the United States and other countries). Details and regions: `infra/sub-processors.md`.

## Planned API mutation receipts (22 September 2026; not enabled)

This is a prospective design entry, **not a change to the data currently collected by the Telegram pilot**. The receipt service, incremental migration and optional isolated account-write routes exist in the repository; since 25 September 2026 staging's pilot Worker serves the API with its writes on and staging's database holds the migration, for test accounts only (staging holds no real person's app account), while production's API is off and its database has not received the migration. Before enabling real response caching, review and version the public notices and integrate account/resource deletion; do not treat this entry as approval to collect new live data.

| Category | Source and storage | Purpose | Retention | Access and processors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| API mutation replay receipts | Server-validated mutation results in `api_request_receipts`: SHA-256 hashes of the auth subject, opaque request key and canonical operation/input/scope envelope; optional family/member IDs; success status and approved JSON response; completion and expiry times. Raw keys, auth subjects, session IDs, request bodies and headers are not copied. Hashes remain pseudonymous personal data; the saved response may contain profile or family data | Retry a database mutation without executing it twice during the replay window | Replay ends 24 hours after completion. The next healthy nightly retention run deletes expired rows, so normal physical retention is at most 48 hours; replay does not extend it. Scoped rows cascade with the referenced family/member. The internal `disableApiAccount` transaction purges actor receipts and retains an access-denial tombstone; verified lifecycle wiring and full profile erasure are still required before activation. Authorized requests also reclaim expired actor receipts; a 1,000-receipt actor cap bounds active cache growth. Resource-creation/deletion handlers must correctly associate and remove cached personal data | Only the currently authorized caller may receive a replay; the founder through controlled database access. No content-bearing receipt access for the coding agent outside synthetic tests. Neon and CF in the operation's database region |

Each endpoint must approve its exact response fields and deletion scopes before using this cache. Tokens, cookies, provider credentials and unreviewed content are not eligible. This is a database transaction mechanism, not exactly-once delivery for Telegram, email, payments or other remote effects. The privacy notice continues to describe the existing pilot until the write API is reviewed and enabled.

## End of the pilot without continuing

Within 30 days the founder requests the family's deletion (`delete_family` on the admin page, flows §3.17, build plan 1.12), and the retention job deletes the family's rows within 24 hours of that request (flows §3.15, build plan 2.8). Until both have shipped, the founder deletes them by hand (`infra/runbooks/data-requests.md` K). Kept longer, each for its own period above: rows 5 (forgotten, 5 years), 16 (without names or output), 17, 18, 19 (5 years), 20 and 22. The retention job forgets the consent rows of every member and contact of the family in the same transaction as the family's deletion. The privacy notice lists the same exceptions.

## What the AI sees

Per spec §17 and architecture §12: the answer (text or transcript), the ask, the family's replies, the member list with first names, roles and languages, memory facts, and for `understand` whether the person has given her health-words consent (`healthWordsConsent`, which tells the model to leave health out without it). Never billing data, never nearby contacts' numbers, never the whole archive. Every call is logged in `ai_calls` with its prompt version (row 16).

## Gaps found while building this map

Each gap says what was found and, where the sprint 1 contract decisions of 14 September 2026 close it, how the design closes it (flows section and decision record) and when it is closed in practice (the build plan task that ships it). Until then the pack's manual steps stand.

1. **Retention the sources left open.** The schema's retention covered media, transcripts, left members, deleted families and events only, and flows §3.15 and build plan 2.8 listed no more, so the job as specified would not have kept the notice's 30-day promise for asks, replies, translations, chips, suggestions, `outbound.payload`, `ai_calls.output` or `message_refs`, nor deleted `metrics_daily` at 24 months. *Closed in the design:* ADR-24 and flows §3.15 put this pack's rules (rows 3, 4, 7–14, 16, 17) into `applyRetention`, and the schema's retention header lists them. A cleared NOT NULL column takes its empty value, and chips and translations rows are deleted, since they hold nothing but their text. *Closed in practice* when `applyRetention` ships with a retention test for every rule and runs nightly (build plan 2.8); until then the notice is not used with any real family (`README.md`, "Before any family"). Still open: gap 18.
2. **Summaries kept while the family uses Vela** leave a derived trace of every answer until the family is deleted, which needed a deletion path at the end of the pilot. *Closed in the design:* the founder requests a family's deletion on the admin page (`delete_family`, flows §3.17, ADR-22) and the retention job deletes the family within 24 hours (flows §3.15, ADR-24). *Closed in practice* when build plan 1.12 and 2.8 have both shipped; until then, `infra/runbooks/data-requests.md` K.
3. **`metrics_daily` was "kept"** in the reference schema while architecture §12 limits events to 24 months; the notice promises 24 months for both. *Closed in the design:* ADR-24 deletes `metrics_daily` rows older than 24 months. *Closed in practice* when `applyRetention` ships (build plan 2.8).
4. **Consent proof** cascaded away when a member was deleted, so proof of consent disappeared with the data. *Closed in the design* on 17 September 2026 (L5, ADR-28, `legal-memo.md` C7 and C8): `consents.member_id` and `contact_id` are `ON DELETE SET NULL`, every deletion of a member or contact first cuts the evidence to ids and the text hash (`consents_subject_deleted_check` refuses a deletion that skips it), and rows no longer in force are deleted after 5 years (row 5). *Closed in practice* when the services and the retention job of that round are proven on staging. Still open: counsel's view on the basis and the period (memo lawyer question 4).
5. **Events partitioning.** Architecture §5 and §12 drop monthly partitions after 24 months; code design §3 makes `events` a plain table. Retention is a delete by `at`, not a partition drop (ADR-24).
6. **Unrelated group messages.** The bot's group privacy mode stays on (flows §1): asks are replies to the evening prompt, `/ask` or `/later`, which privacy mode delivers. But reactions count only when the bot is a group administrator, and Telegram delivers every group message to an administrator bot whatever the privacy setting. The notice says so and promises everything not meant for Vela is discarded without being stored or logged; the inbound handler must do exactly that (flows §5, "anything else: ignore"). The one service message Vela reads is `left_chat_member`, which every bot receives in privacy mode (gap 14).
7. **Golden set in git** (architecture §9.3, build plan "ten real, consented cases" weekly) conflicts with provable deletion (constraint 6): only truly anonymised text may be committed, and voice never.
8. **OpenAI and Groq** receive audio in architecture §9.2 and build plan 2.5 but are missing from the architecture §12 sub-processor list.
9. **Health words** in `answers.mentions`, `flag_reason` and `memory_facts` (kind `health`): whether these are "medical" data under PDPA Article 6 needs counsel's view before memory ships. *Closed in the design* on 17 September 2026 for answers (L1 to L3, ADR-27, `legal-memo.md` C1 to C4): a separate written consent (`health_words`) asked right after the yes to the light; without it no health mentions, no category in `flag_reason`, no words in the organisers' flag notice; for everyone no diagnosis, test result or medicine name in summaries and mentions. Health memory facts only with that consent (row 29, C5). Still open: a relayed diagnosis inside a transcript, which the family group receives as sent and which is deleted after 30 days (memo lawyer question 1).
10. **Pilot research data** (UCLA-3, call notes, the day-30 answer on paying) lives outside the schema, so the tool holding it had to be named in the notice before use. *Closed* on 17 September 2026: it is Google Drive in the founder's own Google account, in a private folder "Vela pilot" shared with no one, filed under a family code (rows 5 and 22), and both notices name it and where its data is stored (Google's servers in the United States and other countries; a personal account offers no choice of location). On the same day (L13, `legal-memo.md` C11) the notes were limited to the family code, dates, text versions, yes or no answers, the "who can join" answers as codes, and questionnaire scores: never quotes, names, contact details or anything about health (row 22). Still open: a personal account comes with Google's own terms and no data processing terms (Google's Cloud Data Processing Addendum covers Google Workspace and Google Cloud customers, not a personal account; `infra/sub-processors.md`); counsel to confirm that minimal notes under a family code may stay there, or the founder moves them to his own encrypted device (memo lawyer question 9).
11. **Nearby contacts at setup.** Flows §3.1 asked the organiser for nearby contacts during setup and inserted them without consent, and §3.12 listed every nearby contact in the quiet notice. *Closed in the design:* flows §3.1 keeps the setup step but stores contacts with `consented_at` null, and §3.12 lists in `quiet.nearby` only contacts with `consented_at` set and `declined_at` null, leaving the line out when there are none (code design §10, "Nearby contacts"); `onboarding.ask_nearby` tells the organiser to ask each contact themselves before their number can appear in any note (already in `@vela/copy`). The founder records each yes or no on the admin page (`record_contact_consent`, flows §3.17). *Closed in practice* when the sprint 1 services ship the quiet notice and the admin actions (build plan 1.8, 1.12); until the admin page ships, the founder records answers by hand (`infra/runbooks/data-requests.md` C). The design does not say what happens to a stored contact who says no or never answers; this pack removes them (row 6, data-requests D). *Narrowed* on 17 September 2026 (L8, `legal-memo.md` C17 and C18): the setup step and `add_contact` without a yes store a name and relation only, and a number arrives only with the recorded yes. Still open: whether storing a name before the yes has a lawful basis (memo lawyer question 5).
12. **Region.** Flows §3.1 set `families.region` from the country (`us`, `eu`), while only the `apac` database exists and the notice says the database is in Singapore. *Closed in the design:* ADR-7 (updated 14 September 2026) and flows §3.1: `Config.regions` lists the regions that exist (`apac` only in the pilot), and a country whose preferred region does not exist falls back to `apac`, so every pilot family is `apac`. *Closed in practice* when the sprint 1 services ship onboarding; the founder confirms the region after the first setups (`infra/runbooks/data-requests.md` A). Before the `eu` or `us` database holds anyone, the notice needs a new version.
13. **No admin write path.** The admin route was a read-only page and no flow wrote `pilot`, `privacy_notice` or `nearby` consents, organiser-set away periods, `deceased`, a member leaving or a family's deletion. *Closed in the design:* ADR-22 and flows §3.17 add admin actions (services module `admin.ts`; POST forms on `/admin` in the admin Worker `vela-admin`, behind Cloudflare Access with a verified Access JWT and a same-origin check, ADR-26): `record_consent`, `record_contact_consent`, `add_contact`, `remove_contact`, `set_away`, `end_away`, `mark_left`, `mark_deceased`, `delete_family` and `send_weekly_read`. Each writes an `admin_access_log` row with its `action` (and `member_id` when it is about one member) and records a domain event; opening a page writes action `view`. On 17 September 2026 `create_invite` was added (L7): a fresh invited profile and invite link for a family whose person said No or never answered, the link sent to the organiser's own chat. *Closed in practice* when the sprint 1 worker ships these forms (build plan 1.12, and 1.14 for `send_weekly_read`); until then the founder follows `infra/runbooks/data-requests.md`, logging each family touched by hand. Still manual afterwards: a new invite link, a copy of someone's information, corrections, and deleting one family member's information on request (data-requests A, H, I, J).
14. **Departures.** Vela received no update when someone left a group, so nothing set `members.left_at`. *Closed in the design:* Telegram delivers the `left_chat_member` service message to bots even in privacy mode, inside `message`, so the webhook's `allowed_updates` needs no `chat_member` (flows §1), and `@vela/adapters` already turns it into a `member_left` event naming who left. In a linked group, a linked member with role `member` who is not the person the light is for becomes `left` with `left_at` and leaves the turn rotation (event `member_left`); an organiser or the person the light is for keeps their state, event `member_left_group` is recorded and the founder receives `admin.member_left_group`; someone with no link leaves nothing stored (flows §3.16; code design §10, "Departures"). *Closed in practice* when the sprint 1 services ship it (build plan 1.11). Not verified: that Telegram posts the service message in every kind of group; the founder still marks a member left when told (`mark_left`).
15. **The admin conversation** (row 25) held verbatim flag quotes and weekly-read drafts outside `admin_access_log` and outside `applyRetention`, and the pack relied on the founder deleting them within 30 days. *Closed in the design:* ADR-21: `admin.flag`, `admin.weekly_read_draft` and `admin.understand_failed` carry the family's name and a link to the Access-protected admin page (`Config.publicBaseUrl` + `/admin/families/<family id>`), never her words, and opening the page writes `admin_access_log` (flows §3.10, §3.14, §3.15, §3.17). The copy keys are already changed in `@vela/copy`. Organisers still receive `flag.notice` with her words. *Closed in practice* when the sprint 1 services and worker ship (build plan 1.9).
16. **AI failures were not retried.** `@vela/ai` resolves every provider error to its safe default, and nothing ran the flag check again. *Closed in the design:* ADR-25 and flows §3.10, §3.15: `answers.understood_at` is set only when `ai.understand` and `ai.flag` both succeed (a failed translation does not block it); `answers.processing_attempts` (already in `@vela/db`) counts each start of media ingestion or understanding, so a voice answer's first run counts two when its transcription succeeds; every 15 minutes (every 5 until the ADR-25 update of 18 September 2026) `reconcile` re-enqueues answers not understood that arrived between 15 minutes and 24 hours ago with fewer than three attempts, and the founder receives `admin.understand_failed` once after the third failed attempt. Re-runs never repeat the group transcript post, translations or flag notices. *Closed in practice* when the sprint 1 services ship it (build plan 1.13); until then, and for any answer that ends in `admin.understand_failed`, the founder reads it by hand (`infra/runbooks/incident.md`).
17. **What the family sees.** Spec §9 promises the last weekly read with the summaries; flows §3.13 returned summaries only, and `weekly_reads` did not hold the text the founder sent. *Closed in the design:* the founder edits the draft on the admin page and taps Send (`send_weekly_read`); `weekly_reads.sent_lines`, `sent_suggestion` and `sent_at` (already in `@vela/db`) keep the lines and suggestion as sent, Vela sends them to each active organiser after the counts it renders from `weekly_reads.stats`, and "what does the family see" returns the summaries of her last seven answered days and the lines of the most recent sent weekly read, never its counts or suggestion (`parent.family_sees_weekly_read`; flows §3.13, §3.14, §3.17; code design §10, "Weekly read"). The notice now says so. *Closed in practice* when the sprint 1 services and worker ship it (build plan 1.14).
18. **The admin access log's 24 months** (row 18) is not in ADR-24's list, so no job deletes older rows. Nothing is due before September 2028; the retention job needs the rule, or the founder deletes older rows by hand, before then.
19. **Benchmark clips' account** (row 23). The consented voice clips were to go to the private R2 bucket `vela-benchmark` in the "Vela staging" Cloudflare account. That is the account the development machine's Wrangler sign-in reaches, and a Wrangler sign-in covers every bucket in its account (`infra/README.md`, access rules), so for these clips the rule that the co-founder never handles real family content would have rested on policy, not on the account, and staging would have held real voices. *Closed* on 17 September 2026 by the founder: the clips are consented personal data from real families, so `vela-benchmark` is created in the "Vela" (production) account, which the co-founder never signs in to, and the clips are out of the co-founder's reach like every other place real family content lives (build plan 2.5).
20. **Where families live.** The map assumes Taiwan's Act alone, while the Act applies to the founder on top of the law where each family member lives, and Russia's law follows citizenship (`legal-memo.md` Q6). *Closed for the pilot* on 17 September 2026 (L12, C23): the founder records each person's country of residence and whether anyone is a Russian citizen before setup, as codes and yes or no in the notes (row 22), and onboards only families living in Taiwan or in US states other than Washington with no Russian citizen (`README.md`, "Who can join the pilot"). Still open: the steps each other place needs before its families join.
21. **Photo objects no row names** (26 September 2026, ADR-33). A photo uploaded from the app is stored in the bucket before its `media` row is written, under a key made for that one upload. A failure the Worker sees removes the object, unless its row may have been written. Three cases still leave an object under `asks/` that no row names: a Worker that stops between the object and the row; a delete that fails (logged as `media_orphan_delete_failed`); and a failure after which the row cannot even be looked for (logged as `media_upload_commit_unknown`). The retention job works from rows and never finds such an object, and nothing serves it. *Backstop:* a lifecycle rule on the bucket deletes objects under `asks/` 32 days after they were written: two days past the notice's 30 for such an object, so that the retention job deletes every photo with a row first and writes its proof. *Not yet set:* the rule is a setting of the bucket, not of the code; it is added to `vela-media-staging` with `wrangler r2 bucket lifecycle add` when this change reaches staging, and to `vela-media-production` before production's API is turned on (`infra/README.md`, section 1, step 3). The same rule would delete a photo the family keeps, so the family book (row 28) must copy the photos it keeps out of `asks/` when it ships.
