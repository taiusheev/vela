## 4. Cultural insights: why elders accept or reject, who pays

- Japan: the adult child is the buyer; the parent is the reluctant "user". Zojirushi's own framing is a tool "maintaining appropriate parent-child distance"; Yamato chose a light bulb because "cameras or sensors make elderly people feel watched"; Docomo's Chikaku sells itself as "a gentler choice than monitoring" and deliberately shows an illustration instead of video. [Zojirushi](https://www.zojirushi.co.jp/corp/news/2023/230314/mimamori.html), [Walkerplus](https://www.walkerplus.com/article/1146473/), [Docomo](https://chikaku.docomo.ne.jp/column/25040005/)
- Purchase is reactive: 34% of families adopted only after a home accident; 24% on a care-manager's recommendation. Top non-use reasons: "don't know what these services actually do" (31%) and cost (28%). Current users pay a median ¥0-300/mo; prospects say ¥1,001-2,000. Cost is the #1 consideration for 75.9%. [SMS](https://www.bm-sms.co.jp/news-press/prs_20230720_ansinkaigo_research/), [Fudousan Japan](https://www.fudousan.or.jp/topics/1212/12_3.html)
- Elder objections (MyVoice): privacy, "we don't know what we'd do if an alert fires", loss of independence, distrust of staff holding personal data; "non-video" and "alert-only-on-anomaly" designs are now mainstream. [Senior Life Soken](https://seniorlife-soken.com/archives/47413), [Sunlove](https://www.sunlove.jp/mimamori/cam-privacy/)
- Korea: the state, not the child, pays. Programs are welfare targeted at low-income solitary elders (기초수급/차상위), delivered by municipalities and telco CSR. Emotional framing works: NUGU users' top use is companionship, and 38% of counselling is emotional; Hyodol sells as a "grandchild". [SKT](https://news.sktelecom.com/195555), [AI Times](https://www.aitimes.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=38607)
- China: filial duty is codified (Elderly Rights Law) but 空巢 is >50%; the community (社区/街道) is the response layer, not the child; children are the "emergency contact" in the water-meter workflow, with volunteers as fallback. Consent forms are explicitly collected. [Jing'an](https://www.jingan.gov.cn/rmtzx/003008/003008005/20230823/725590b7-65db-4006-b4dd-574cc59f4121.html)
- HK/SG/TW: charity + subsidy model; free for means-tested solitary elders, paid for others; public-housing infrastructure (HDB, rental blocks) is the distribution channel.

## 5. What has worked at scale and why

1. Government-funded, welfare-targeted deployments dwarf consumer products: Korea 300k households (응급안전안심), Seoul 4k+ smart plugs, Shanghai 9k water meters in one year, Singapore 26.8k seniors, HK 4k free Safety Bells — versus Zojirushi's 14k paying subscribers after 24 years and Yamato's ~9k.
2. Piggyback on infrastructure people already have: water meter, smart electricity meter (KEPCO ¥0), light bulb, kettle, postman, delivery driver. Zero-install, no Wi-Fi, no smartphone for the elder is the common denominator of every product that gained traction.
3. Pair detection with a human response layer: Yamato driver visits, Seoul welfare planners, Shanghai volunteers, SCHSA nurses, Korea auto-119. Sensors alone stall because families "don't know what to do with an alert".
4. AI voice calls scale cheapest: CareCall reached 30k users across 56% of Korean municipalities in 3 years with 96% answer rates, because it replaces manual welfare calls rather than adding hardware. [Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20240905000213)
5. Companionship framing beats surveillance framing for elder acceptance and measurable outcomes (SKT happiness/depression shifts; Hyodol -35.7% depression high-risk).

## 6. Gaps

- Alert precision: Seoul logged 46,974 signals for 154 real interventions (0.3%); municipal staff absorb the noise. No consumer product publishes false-positive rates.
- Japan's child-payer market is fragmented across ~45 services with no integrated "one app for my parent" layer; awareness, not technology, is the bottleneck (31% don't understand offerings).
- Middle-income elders (not poor enough for Korean/Chinese/HK subsidy, not wealthy enough for Secom's ¥48k-195k setup) are underserved everywhere.
- 40% of kodokushi are under 60; nearly all programs are 65+ gated.
- Consent and privacy: water-meter and camera programs require opt-in; refusal rates aren't published in any market found.
- Price expectation gap in Japan (¥1-2k/mo wanted vs ¥3-5k for full service) suggests sub-¥1,000 utility-bundled offerings (KEPCO ¥0, au ¥539, Chubu ¥550) are where growth is going.
