## 2. Per-category findings

### (a) Daily "I'm OK" check-in apps
**What works**
- Dead simple, mostly free, and elders self-enroll (Snug's 1,800-customer survey: 94.6% live alone, 77% female, 82% "very disappointed if they didn't have Snug"). Reviews are overwhelmingly written by the elder, not the child: "Every time I check in on this little app, I am flooded with gratitude and peace of mind" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/snug-safety/id1122758716?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)).
- Escalation is the product. Paid tier ($12.50/mo) buys a human dispatcher; a reviewer reports a dispatcher call and a police welfare check "within 30 minutes" of a missed check-in ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/snugsafe.com)).
**What fails**
- Nagging: "starts beeping at 8:30 until I check the box… way to early reminders"; app breakage "Says it can't check you in… uninstalled permanently"; billing disputes over $17.99 multi-check-in tier; unresponsive support ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/snugsafe.com)).
- Phone-call variants get screened: Iamfine is "a poor match" if the parent "screens unknown numbers, ignores calls" and calls "can feel like spam" ([CheckRise review](https://www.checkrise.co/blog/iamfine-review/)).
- Amazon's $19.99/mo Alexa Together (activity feed, remote assist, 10-caregiver circle) was shut down and replaced by a ~$5.99 emergency-only product — the strongest signal that a big-brand passive-monitoring subscription at $20/mo did not retain enough families.
**Retention math (back-of-envelope)**: Snug's "30M+ check-ins" over ~10 years ≈ 8K check-ins/day, i.e. roughly 5–10K daily active elders for the category leader. Iamfine's "4M+ calls" over 14 years ≈ 800 calls/day ≈ 400–800 active subscribers. The daily-tap category is real but tiny; the sub-$15 price and dozens of free clones cap it.

### (b) AI voice check-in / companion calls
**Evidence AI conversation is accepted by elders (strongest to weakest)**
1. **CareCall (Korea, B2G)**: Busan pilot: 94.9% wanted to continue, 90% "felt consoled" ([Naver](https://www.navercorp.com/en/media/pressReleasesDetail?seq=30782)); nationwide "96% of all users respond", ~90% satisfaction ([AVING](https://us.aving.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=51181)); Naver/Yonsei: 44.2% fewer solitary deaths, 9.2% fewer ER visits in served localities ([Pickool](https://www.thepickool.com/naver-ai-carecall-reduces-solitary-deaths-by-44-2/)). RCT-lite: 80 dementia patients, twice-weekly calls averaging only ~1.5 min, GDS depression 8.5→6.0, memory 3→4, no control group ([Sci Reports 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12297396/)).
   - Counter-evidence from the same product: in Jeongeup (n=2,896, weekly calls) users completed only ~3 calls in 3–4 months (≈20% per-call completion), 34–77% of domain questions went unasked, and the system missed "I feel like there's no way out. Maybe I should just die" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12924473/)).
2. **Meela (senior living, free to resident)**: 50%+ retention at 90 days, 15-min average calls, half >20 min; 83-year-old: "I'm used to now saying 'she.' At first, I used to insist on saying 'it.'" ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/ai-companionship-for-seniors-bronx)); spouse of early-Alzheimer's user: "Larry has always confided to me that he has difficulty initiating conversations – AI takes care of that" ([ThriveMore](https://www.thrivemorenc.org/news/ai-with-heart)).
3. **ElliQ (NY state, free to user)**: 3,500+ applied for ~900 free units; 2026 report: 94% "feel less lonely", 4.6/5 satisfaction, but only **4 interactions/day** average (vs. "30+/day" claimed in 2023) and **60% retention after 3 months** on the coaching feature ([NYSOFA 2026 PDF](https://aging.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/02/nysofa-elliq-project-update-2026.pdf)). Paying-customer review: 2/5, "interactions last ~2 lines", freezes, reviewer "felt empty" ([Senior List](https://www.theseniorlist.com/aging-in-place/elliq/)).
4. **Callie Care**: 85% of *engaged* users asked to keep getting calls (survivor-biased) ([AI Journal](https://aijourn.com/callie-care-raises-500k-pre-seed-to-tackle-americas-senior-care-gap-with-phone-first-ai/)).
5. **inTouch (B2C, €29.90)**: journalist test — voice "too fast and business-like", like "automated company cold calls"; tester's mother: "I would feel terrible and that they are not bothered about phoning me" ([404 Media via ISPR](https://ispr.info/2025/04/15/i-tested-the-ai-that-calls-your-elderly-parents-if-you-cant-bothered/)); columnist calls it "dystopian, insulting" ([Cocotte Minute](https://cocotteminute.substack.com/p/the-ai-that-calls-your-elderly-parents)). Founder counters that seniors know it's AI and don't object.

**Pattern**: Every AI-voice deployment with real numbers is paid by a government, Medicaid, or a senior-living operator. No B2C AI-call product has published retention. Human-companion analog (Papa) blew up on trust/safety (1,200 complaints) and payer churn.

**Business-model attrition**: Mon Ami (companion visits → AAA software), Carefull (consumer $29/mo → bank channel), Grayce (dead → Cariloop), Meela (companion → rides on its homepage), Sensi (never B2C). The pattern: consumer eldercare wallets are thin; survivors sell to institutions.

**Cost tailwind**: AloneAssist explicitly prices AI calls at the $14.99 IVR level because "voice AI pricing dropped sharply in 2024 and 2025" ([AgeInPlaceTech](https://www.ageinplacetech.com/pressrelease/aloneassist-launches-ai-powered-daily-wellness-call-older-adults-1499-month)) — price compression toward $15 is already happening; ElderVoice cut its daily plan from $49 to $25.

### (c) Family coordination
- Coordination-only apps are free forever (Lotsa, CaringBridge nonprofit) or employer-paid (Wellthy, Cariloop, ianacare, Homethrive). CareZone died inside Walmart; Grayce folded. Nobody has proven a family will pay a subscription for coordination alone.
- **Financial safety is the one adjacent B2C product with a $29/mo price point** (Carefull) — but it moved to distributing through banks/advisors "free of charge" to the end family, again signalling consumer price resistance. EverSafe holds at $7.49–26.99.

### (d) Elder devices / smart speakers
- Devices that persist charge **hardware + $10–65/mo** and are bought by adult children (GrandPad $40–59/mo "great… but too darn expensive"; ViewClix $9.95/mo; Lively plans). The buyer values *administration* (GrandPad Family Circle, RAZ remote control, Lively Link).
- Alexa Together lesson: generic-platform caregiving subscriptions can't justify $20/mo when the base device already does calls/reminders; Amazon kept only the emergency layer.
- Hyodol: 12,000 units, gov't-funded; social worker: "With a companion greeting them at the door, some trap themselves in their homes"; a dementia patient wandered to a creek after the doll mentioned stream sounds; aide workload rose ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2025/korea-ai-robot-senior-care-hyodol/)).

### (e) Loneliness robots/companions
- Evidence is real but soft: Paro calms dementia patients; Lovot shows cortisol reduction (Shiseido study); ElliQ/Hyodol/Meela all report self-rated loneliness drops. Critiques (Turkle, Carpenter): illusion of relationship, isolation deepening, opaque data use.

## 3. Will families pay $20–50/month? What the evidence says
- **Yes, for hardware-anchored bundles bought by the child**: GrandPad ($40–59/mo) and Lively have survived a decade at that level.
- **Weak/no for software-only**: Alexa Together ($19.99) discontinued; Carefull ($29) moved to bank channel; ElderVoice discounted 49→25; category pricing anchoring at $14.99 (Iamfine, CheckRise, AloneAssist, Snug $12.50). ElliQ B2C at $30–40/mo + $249 exists but its published traction is entirely subsidized (3,500 applicants for free units).
- **Yes, at institutional level**: Korean municipalities (50K users), NY State ($2.9M line item), Washington Medicaid, senior-living operators absorbing Meela's cost, home-care agencies buying Sensi ($98M raised). The proven payer for AI check-ins is a government or operator, not the daughter.

## 4. Gaps and opportunities
1. **No one has published B2C retention for AI calls.** The first product to show 6-month paid retention data on families (not residents) would own the narrative. Callie's "85% of engaged users" and Meela's 50%/90-day (free) are the only public numbers.
2. **The "parent screens the call" problem** is unsolved: IVR calls read as spam; Apple Check In is trip-based; the fix is likely caller-ID branding plus a known number, or piggybacking on an existing channel (WhatsApp/LINE voice notes, landline like Community Phone's 30K base).
3. **Hybrid escalation is the moat, not the LLM.** Snug's paid tier and CareCall's social-worker handoff are what users praise; CareCall's missed suicidal cue and Hyodol's wandering incident show pure-AI monitoring creates liability. A $20–30 tier that includes a human dispatcher/welfare-check path is the only software-only offer with evidence of willingness to pay.
4. **Financial + safety bundle**: Carefull ($29) and daily check-in ($12.50) each sit below $30; nobody bundles fraud-monitoring + wellness signal + coordination into one family plan — the adult child's actual anxiety set.
5. **Price compression is coming from below**: voice-AI cost collapse means $14.99 AI calls (AloneAssist) will commoditize "daily call"; differentiation must be in longitudinal signal (mood/cognition trends, memory of prior calls — Nonni's "52 emotional measures", Callie's "44% of calls reveal health info") and in actionability for the child.
6. **Distribution reality**: YC's only elder "peace of mind" plays (Community Phone W19, GoGoGrandparent S16, True Link S13) are all phone/landline/card-based utilities, not apps; there's no LLM-era YC voice-companion for elders in the directory. Institutional channels (AAAs via Mon Ami, Medicaid via ElliQ, senior living via Meela, AARP AgeTech via JoyCalls) are where 2025–26 revenue is actually flowing.
7. **Dignity framing matters**: the most cited elder objection ("they are not bothered about phoning me") argues for positioning AI calls as *the elder's* tool (Snug-style self-enrolment, independence framing — Snug's users call themselves "very independent") rather than the child's surveillance tool.
