## 3. Country-by-country model summary

**UK — council-procured, migrating to private-pay.** ~1.7–2M users ([TSA](https://www.telecare.org.uk/about-us), [Care England](https://www.careengland.org.uk/digital-switchover-telecare-impact/)); 30–50% already pay privately ([LGA](https://www.local.gov.uk/sites/default/files/documents/telecare-and-assistive-te-e36_0.pdf)). 51% of over-75s in England live alone. Councils win/lose via tender to Tunstall/Careium/Appello, then charge users ~£4–6/wk; austerity is pushing councils to "private-pay pathways" (Taking Care's explicit product). PSTN switch-off (now Jan 2027) is the forcing event: 2.3–11.5% call failure on analogue-over-digital, two deaths in 2023, 3 of 7 largest providers still selling analogue kit to consumers ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/closing-the-gaps-protecting-telecare-users-in-the-shift-from-analogue-to-digital-azmhv4i4Uknj)). Only ~25% of housing schemes were digital-ready by 2025 ([AT Today](https://attoday.co.uk/more-than-750000-users-could-be-reliant-on-outdated-telecare-technology-by-2025/)). Highest 75+ penetration in Europe (~36%).

**Germany — insurer-reimbursed flat rate.** Pflegekasse pays €25.50/mo for Hausnotruf to anyone with Pflegegrad 1+ who lives (mostly) alone; this fixed number has become the market price. ~1.2M users, ~95% via the five welfare associations. Adoption barrier: you must first have a Pflegegrad and file a request; premium/mobile features are out-of-pocket. Startups (Patronus, Helfi-Ruf, Gisela) are private-pay and do not sit in the reimbursement code.

**Nordics — municipal entitlement.** Sweden: needs-assessed (biståndsbeslut), ~210–223k alarms, 97% stationary; fee 0–528 kr/mo inside the national maxtaxa cap; municipalities procure via multi-year frameworks (SKL/Adda), Careium waiting on "a new major tender framework" in 2025. Norway similar, ~535 NOK/mo with income-based waiver, Oslo alone worth SEK 50M/yr to Careium. Finland: wellbeing counties price 50–70 €/mo with Vivago (circadian-rhythm watch) and AddSecure as suppliers. Startups sell to municipalities, not families; 2G/3G sunset delayed Nordic replacement cycles.

**Netherlands — fragmented payer.** Zvw covers the device only on strict medical indication and never the monthly fee; Wmo is per-municipality; so most families pay. Result: a mature D2C market (Sensara €15–17.50/mo, Compaan tablet €299 + €12.95/mo) and B2B sales to thuiszorg organisations.

**France — B2C with tax-credit subsidy.** >900k subscribers but only 10–12% of 75+ equipped, growing 2–3%/yr. 50% tax credit + APA make it cheap, yet 87% of relatives see it as a "marker of dependency". AFRATA lobbies for 20% by 2030 and a dedicated levy.

**Spain — the state-funded exemplar.** 934k publicly funded users (2021), ~70% of cost borne by administrations, user co-pay €0–12/mo, private alternatives €18–35/mo. Contracts are large regional/provincial tenders (Barcelona Diputación 70k users) won by CLECE, Tunstall Televida, DomusVi, EULEN; ~19% of 75+ equipped, extreme regional variance (Castilla-La Mancha 16.3 vs Valencia 1.0 coverage index).

**Italy — thin.** Telesoccorso is regionally subsidised in places, otherwise private; "telecompagnia" (scheduled human calls) is an established tier — the human predecessor of AI check-ins.

**Israel — welfare-funded floor + strong B2B tech.** ~20k state-funded buttons via Yad Sarah for 75+ living alone on income support; Natali dominates private. Israeli companies (Essence, Vayyar, EchoCare) sell radar/IoT hardware to European and US telecare operators rather than to Israeli families.

**Australia — federal package funding.** From Nov 2025 the Support at Home AT-HM scheme ring-fences alarm funding (tiers up to $15k) so it no longer competes with cleaning/meals; providers must be registered. Retail D2C also strong (LiveLife $567 up-front + $90/yr; Sofihub $1,199).

**New Zealand — price-capped accredited suppliers.** WINZ Disability Allowance funds most alarms if a GP certifies need; accredited suppliers must charge $13–15/wk; market consolidated to 5 firms; mis-selling is the main complaint.

## 4. Business-model insights

**How startups get paid in state-funded markets**
1. Win the framework tender (SE/NO/FI/ES/UK councils): multi-year, price-per-user-per-month, replacement cycles tied to 2G/3G/PSTN sunsets. High cost of sales, slow, but 74%+ recurring revenue and 40%+ gross margin once in (Careium).
2. Sell hardware/software to the incumbents (Essence, Vayyar, Nobi, Enovation) — the Israeli route.
3. Fit the reimbursement code exactly (Germany €25.50; NZ $13–15/wk; Spain co-pay bands) — flat rates cap ARPU but remove buyer friction.
4. Bypass the state and sell to the adult child (UK Careline365, NL Sensara, FR Présence Verte, DE Helfi-Ruf, AI-call startups). Price band is remarkably consistent: €15–30/mo, which is also what public co-pays land at.
5. Hybrid "private-pay pathway": councils refer users they de-fund to a private provider (Taking Care).

**Churn / retention** — no operator publishes it. Structural churn is driven by death and care-home entry: median user is a woman 80+ living alone (74% of Spanish users; UK majority women 70+ alone), implying annual attrition on the order of 15–25% and ~4–6 year tenures. Private-pay providers hedge with annual prepay (Careline365 £125–205/yr, LiveLife 12-month bundle), 12–24 month contracts with 2–3 month penalties (Spain), and low-cost hardware. Non-use ("device in a drawer") is the second churn mode; no reliable public figures.

**Uptake among 75+ living alone** — UK ~36%, Spain ~19%, France 10–12% of all 75+; Germany 1.2M users vs ~8M 75+ suggests ~15% (author's estimate); Sweden ~215k alarms vs ~1M 75+ ≈ 20%. Even in the best market two-thirds of the target population is unserved, and the served population skews to already-dependent 80+ women. France's data point that 87% of families see telecare as a dependency stigma is the key demand-side barrier; supply-side barriers are needs assessment gates (SE, NL Wmo, DE Pflegegrad), analogue/2G legacy, and reactive-only design (2% of calls are emergencies — 98% are reassurance/social).

## 5. Gaps and opportunities for a global startup

1. **The "pre-dependency" 70–80 segment is unserved everywhere** — telecare is designed and stigmatised for 80+ frail users; France explicitly wants prevention-positioned products to reach 20%.
2. **98% of alarm-centre traffic is non-emergency** — Italian "telecompagnia" and the AI-call wave (inTouch €29.90, Helfi-Ruf €15–40, Nonni A$40, Call Knut) show demand for proactive contact, but none has scale or a reimbursement code; the field is fragmented, single-language, and family-pay only. A multilingual product priced at the €25 public co-pay band with escalation to family could win D2C now and tender later.
3. **Digital switchover is a forced replacement cycle** — UK (Jan 2027), Nordic 2G/3G, Spain digitalisation (La Rioja/Tunstall 2026): councils are re-tendering and rethinking; incumbents are hardware-heavy and slow.
4. **Payer plumbing is the moat, not the tech**: get listed under Germany's €25.50 code, Australia's AT-HM registered-provider list, NZ accreditation, Spain/Sweden frameworks. Israeli hardware firms and Careium prove B2B2C channel works; nobody yet has a pan-European software-only entrant in the tenders.
5. **Complaint themes to design against**: device reliability and post-sale support (Careline365), doorstep mis-selling and opaque terms (NZ ADT/VitalCall), power/broadband outage failures (Which?), alarms that only work at home (Sweden: 97% stationary).
6. **Capital**: Europe funds care-ops (Cera $150M, Birdie $30M) and hardware (Nobi €35M, Vayyar $108M); AI check-in/companion startups have raised only seed money (Call Knut SEK 2M), so the category is open.
