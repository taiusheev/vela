# 13 — Evidence for the worry and the reach: how many families, how intense, what they do today

Date: 13 September 2026. Method: 21 web searches (English, Russian, Chinese) plus targeted fetches of primary sources — Rosstat, MOI/MOHW Taiwan, ONS, Destatis, ACL/Census, Statistics Korea, AARP/NAC, Carers UK, Age UK, and peer-reviewed/NGO studies. Numbers are quoted as published; anything derived, extrapolated, or a founder assumption is marked **[assumption]** or **[estimate]**. No number below is invented; where a search budget ran out before a figure could be sourced, it is listed in Section 5 as a gap rather than guessed.

## 1. Answer in five lines

1. The elders-living-alone base is large and growing everywhere Vela is looking: **~33% of Russia's pensioner-age population** lives alone (2021 census, up from 24% in 2010), **23.3% of Taiwan's 4.19M elderly** are registered as living alone (2023, ~977,000 people) against a total 65+ population that just crossed 4.7M in 2025, **19.4% of Japan's 65+** live alone today (rising to 20.6%/10.8M by 2050), and Korea, Germany, UK and the US all sit in the **20–34% living-alone range** for 65+, each with hard published figures below.
2. Direct survey evidence of "worry about a parent living alone" specifically does not exist for Russia or Taiwan — VCIOM/Levada and Taiwan's own TLSA/MOHW surveys measure contact patterns, not worry — but adjacent worry data is large and real: **6.6 million UK 40–60-year-olds** say they wouldn't know how to support an aging parent (Age UK, 2023), and **63 million Americans** (1 in 4 adults) now provide family care, up ~50% since 2015 (AARP/NAC, July 2025).
3. What families do today is asymmetric and low-tech relative to the stakes: family group chat is the default contact channel (LINE penetration 93% in Taiwan; 83% of a UK sample check a family group chat daily), phone/text is the fallback for distance, and only ~15% of US caregivers (~5 million people) are classed "long-distance," reporting the highest financial cost (~$12k/yr) and the most emotional distress (47%) of any caregiver group.
4. The Russian-speaking wave-1 diaspora is real but the money rails to reach parents in Russia are largely cut: **650,000–920,000 people left Russia since February 2022** (multiple independent trackers), concentrated in Kazakhstan, Serbia, Armenia, Turkey, Montenegro, Georgia and Israel — but Western remittance apps (Wise, Remitly, Xoom, Revolut) do not serve Russia, and KoronaPay/Zolotaya Korona was sanctioned in Europe in 2024, meaning **messaging apps, not payment apps, are the only realistic distribution rail** into this market.
5. Taiwan is a cleaner but smaller wave 2: it became a **super-aged society in 2025/26** (65+ >20% of 23.3M), LINE is near-universal (93% penetration, with a distinct "elder-image" 長輩圖 greeting-card culture as the dominant elder-to-family message format), and there is a large, government-acknowledged **measurement gap** between the Interior Ministry's registration count of elderly living alone (~977,000) and the Health Ministry's active-outreach caseload (only ~50,000) — precisely the gap a family-facing product could fill.

## 3. Bottom-up market sizing

**Wave 1 — Russian-speaking diaspora with a parent living alone.** Starting point: **650,000–920,000 people left Russia since February 2022** (row 4–5; midpoint ≈ **800,000**, treating this as the "wave" population rather than the larger, older Russian-speaking diaspora already settled abroad, e.g. Germany's 600k+ established community, which is a separate, colder audience). Apply three assumptions, each labeled:
- **[assumption, unsourced]** ~70–75% of this cohort is of an age where a parent is plausibly still alive (emigration skews toward working-age adults in their late 20s–40s per qualitative studies in row 8, but no age-stratified published breakdown was found) → **~560,000–690,000** emigrants with at least one living parent.
- **[sourced proxy, row 2]** ~33% of Russia's pensioner-age population lives alone (2021 census) → applying this national rate to the emigrant cohort's parents (an imperfect proxy — emigrants' parents may skew more urban/educated than the census average) → **~185,000–230,000** families where the parent *already* lives alone today.
- This ~200,000-family figure is the **acute wave-1 TAM**: families with a current, sharpened version of "you'll know the moment it goes quiet." A larger but unquantified SAM sits behind it — parents not yet living alone but with only one adult child, or with all children abroad — which this research could not size for lack of a direct survey (see Gaps).

**Wave 2 — Taiwan.** Starting point: **4.7 million people aged 65+** (2025/26, row 11), of whom **23.3% are registered as living alone** (row 9; using the 2023 rate on the current population ≈ **1.05–1.1 million** people **[estimate: rate held constant across 2023→2025]**, versus the ~977,000 actually reported for 2023 on a smaller elderly base). Apply:
- **[assumption, unsourced]** ~50% of these living-alone elderly have their nearest adult child in a different city or abroad rather than nearby enough for daily in-person contact (no direct Taiwan statistic found; Taiwan's urbanization toward Taipei/New Taipei/Taichung/Kaohsiung is well documented qualitatively but not quantified in this search pass) → **~500,000–550,000 families** as Taiwan's wave-2 addressable population.
- This is likely conservative: MOHW's own active-outreach caseload of only ~50,000 (row 10) against MOI's ~977,000 registered count shows the government itself cannot reach most of this population — the gap a family-facing product is built to close.

Both figures should be read as **order-of-magnitude planning inputs**, not validated TAM/SAM numbers — the founder's decision not to run surveys means these rest on published base rates plus explicit, flagged assumptions rather than primary research.

## 4. Reach plan implications

- **Russia/CIS: messaging, not money rails.** Because Western remittance apps don't serve Russia and KoronaPay is sanctioned in Europe (row 7), the remittance-app distribution channel proposed in the brief is largely closed for wave 1 — a real, useful negative finding. Telegram-based relocant communities (Georgia, Armenia, Serbia, Kazakhstan, Israel per row 4) remain the plausible acquisition channel, but this pass could not size individual community/channel membership before the search budget ran out (see Gaps) — a fast follow-up.
- **Taiwan: build on LINE, don't compete with it.** At 93% penetration and status as the country's default family channel (row 13), a standalone app faces high switching cost; a LINE Official Account / mini-app integration matches how families already exchange the "elder-image" daily greeting and would ride the existing behavior rather than replace it.
- **Philippines (later wave): remittance apps are a real channel, unlike Russia.** GCash's 76M users and Wise's 12% share of inward remittances (row 31) show a live, government-sanctioned rail actively reaching OFW families — the opposite situation to Russia, and worth revisiting when Vela expands beyond wave 1–2.
- **Everywhere: the UK/US worry data (rows 24, 27–28, 38) is the best proxy for message-market fit**, since no direct "worry about a parent living alone" survey exists for Russia or Taiwan. It should be used cautiously in messaging (as directional evidence of intensity), not cited as if it were Russian- or Taiwanese-specific.

## 5. Gaps

- No VCIOM/Levada (or any Russian pollster) survey was found quantifying adult-child contact frequency or worry about elderly parents specifically — this is a real gap, not just a search miss; Levada's "foreign agent" status may also limit what gets published domestically.
- Telegram community sizes for relocant hubs (row 4's destination countries) were not obtained — the WebSearch budget was exhausted mid-session before this could be pursued; a follow-up pass should target this directly.
- Kerala/Gulf-migration elderly-parent data (relevant to a future India/Gulf wave) was identified (a 2025/26 qualitative study on "care beyond co-residence" in Kerala) but its full text sat behind a PubMed cookie wall and could not be extracted this session.
- India and CIS-specific remittance-app user counts (Zolotaya Korona, Unistream registered users) were not found; only their sanctions/access status was confirmed.
- Taiwan's own MOI-vs-MOHW definitional gap (row 10) means the "true" number of isolated elderly is contested even within the Taiwanese government — treat 977,000 as an upper registration-based estimate, not a validated headcount of people at risk.
- No single source reconciles Russian emigration figures (650k–920k range, rows 4–6) or return-migration rates (10% vs. 40–45%, row 6) — country-by-country counting methods differ (residence permits vs. registrations vs. border crossings), so wave-1 sizing above should be treated as a planning range, not a precise count.
