# Evidence: What Families Pay for Peace of Mind and Connection

Research date: 2026-09-13. Compiled from published filings, company pages, and industry reports (WebSearch/WebFetch). No numbers invented; estimates and low-confidence figures are flagged inline.

## 1. Answer in five lines

1. Families do pay for peace-of-mind and connection products, but the "family exchange" category is winner-take-most: Life360 alone monetizes ~2.8–3.2M paying circles at $136–143/year ARPPC (SEC filings, 2025–26) and reportedly out-earns the next-largest family-safety app (Bark, ~$5.3M) by 50x, while gentler connection apps (Tinybeans, Famileo, FamilyAlbum) each sit at $60–100/year ARPU with tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands of paying households.
2. Vela's planned $9.99/month or $79/year sits squarely inside the proven band — below Marco Polo Family ($159.99/yr), close to FamilyAlbum Premium Pro (~$109/yr) and Owlet360 ($9.99/mo), and far below GrandPad ($480–780/yr) or medical-alert PERS ($240–1,080/yr) — so price itself is not the risk.
3. The closest direct analog, Snug Safety (free daily check-in app for isolated seniors, paid $19.99/mo tier that adds a human dispatcher who acts on a missed check-in), validates Vela's exact free/paid split — free thread, paid "someone acts on silence" — at roughly double Vela's price, with 20M+ check-ins completed and no disclosed conversion rate.
4. Freemium conversion, not willingness-to-pay, is the real risk: RevenueCat's cross-category data puts median freemium trial-to-paid conversion at ~2% versus ~11% for hard paywalls, and Social/Lifestyle (Vela's closest app-store category) has the weakest retention of any vertical — so the free layer needs to run for months on habitual daily use before a meaningful share converts.
5. Underlying ability-to-pay is not in question — AARP finds family caregivers already spend $7,242/year out-of-pocket, and diaspora remittances (Philippines $35.6B in 2025; India $138B in 2024) show large sums already move toward family support — but almost none of the published evidence isolates spend specifically on a check-in/notification software layer, so Vela's price has strong adjacent-category support and thin direct-category proof.

## 3. What this means for Vela's pricing and free/paid line

**Keep the $9.99/mo or $79/yr price — it is validated by adjacent categories, not by direct proof.** It undercuts Marco Polo Family ($159.99/yr), lands near FamilyAlbum Premium Pro (~$109/yr) and Owlet360 ($9.99/mo), and is a rounding error against AARP's $7,242/yr average caregiver out-of-pocket spend — Vela Light at $79/yr is ~1.1% of what a typical family caregiver already spends. Nothing in this research suggests price is the constraint; do not test lower before testing the free-to-paid trigger itself.

**Structure the paywall as an escalation, not a feature-gate.** Snug Safety is the single closest comparable in this research: free daily check-in, paid tier adds a human dispatcher who acts when a check-in is missed. That is structurally identical to Vela's plan (free ask/answer thread, paid "family acts on silence" / quiet notice). The market's proven willingness to pay in this category — medical alert ($20–90/mo), Snug ($19.99/mo), Life360's paid safety tiers — attaches to the safety-net *action*, not to cosmetic features (more asks per day, nicer photos, extra storage). Anchor Vela Light specifically to the silence-escalation and quiet-notice mechanic, and resist bundling in feature upsells that dilute that story.

**Price per protected parent, not per family seat.** The classic family-plan literature (Spotify, YouTube, Microsoft 365) is about splitting one shared subscription among household members who each get their own use of the same product — a logic that doesn't map to Vela, where value is generated by one at-risk parent and consumed by multiple children who each want visibility. GrandPad, Life360 (per circle), and medical alert (per protected senior) already price this way. Vela should keep "per protected parent" as the billing unit regardless of how many siblings/relatives join that parent's circle for free — mirroring Life360's paying-circle model rather than a Spotify-style per-seat discount, which would quietly cap revenue as families invite more relatives.

**Assume the adult child is the payer, not the parent.** GrandPad, Storyworth, and Famileo are structurally gift/setup purchases made by an adult child or grandchild on behalf of a parent; GrandPad's own support complaints (e.g., a family unable to cleanly cancel after a parent's death) confirm billing and account control sit with the child, not the elder. Vela's checkout, plan management, and cancellation flows should be designed and tested with the adult child as the account owner and admin by default.

**Default to annual billing.** RevenueCat's Y1 retention gap is stark — 44.1% (annual) vs. 17.0% (monthly) vs. 3.4% (weekly) — and Social/Lifestyle, Vela's closest app-store category, already has the weakest retention of any vertical. The existing $79/yr plan should be the default checkout choice (pre-selected, as RevenueCat notes 60–70% of users choose annual when it's the default), with monthly framed explicitly as a low-commitment trial ramp rather than a primary plan.

**Expect slow, not fast, freemium conversion — plan around engagement, not a day-30 target.** Freemium median trial-to-paid conversion is ~2% versus ~11% for hard paywalls (RevenueCat), and Tinybeans — a fully freemium, years-old family-photo app — has converted only 51–93K paying households from a much larger free base. Vela's free family-exchange thread should be expected to need many months of daily-habit use (the "ask answered daily" loop) before a meaningful share of families convert to Light. Track daily-ask-answered rate and quiet-notice trigger frequency as leading indicators, not day-30 or day-60 conversion percentages.

**Watch for an institutional/B2B2C channel alongside direct subscription.** Papa (Medicare Advantage/employer-funded, $1.4B valuation) and Ianacare (free consumer layer, paid via employer/health-plan add-on) both show that a meaningful share of "family peace of mind" spend already flows through insurers, employers, or telecoms rather than a credit card in the App Store. Taiwan's NT$40B/year LTC 2.0/3.0 budget and its explicit smart-device subsidy (raised to NT$60,000 per 3 years) suggest a similar institutional channel may exist there. This doesn't change Vela's near-term direct-to-consumer price, but it's worth tracking as a distribution option once the direct product is proven.

**Do not assume $9.99/$79 transfers unchanged to Taiwan or Russian-speaking-diaspora markets.** No consumer willingness-to-pay figure was found for either market specific to a check-in app. Famileo's UK range (£5.99–£17.99/mo) and Taiwan's government-subsidized rather than consumer-priced elder-tech model are the only local anchors available; local pricing in those markets should be treated as unvalidated until tested directly.

## 4. Gaps

- **No published free-to-paid conversion percentage exists for any close comparable.** Life360, Tinybeans, Snug, and FamilyAlbum all disclose absolute paying-subscriber counts, not conversion rates. A rough, low-confidence proxy for Life360 (paying circles ÷ MAU) is ~3%, but a "circle" holds multiple members, so this is not a true per-user conversion rate and should not be relied on as one.
- **No survey found isolates spending on a check-in/notification app specifically**, as distinct from total caregiving costs, hired care, or medical-alert hardware. This is the single biggest gap relative to Vela's exact question — the evidence here supports "families have the budget" but not "families have paid for this specific product category before."
- **No Age UK, German, or Japanese survey with a direct $/£/€ willingness-to-pay figure was found.** Searches on Japan/Korea returned academic studies on caregiving burden and support *type* (useful qualitatively, e.g., the Korean 60%/25%/11% split) but no monetary WTP figures; Germany returned no caregiving-spend survey at all.
- **No data on Russian/CIS diaspora spending specifically on parent-care services** (pharmacy delivery, care-coordination apps) was found — only aggregate remittance/GDP-dependency figures for Central Asia. This is a direct gap against the brief's ask about Russian-speaking emigrants.
- **Taiwan data found is about government LTC subsidy structure, not consumer willingness-to-pay for a commercial app**, and no information on Chunghwa Telecom-branded elder services or a "Jubo" LINE-based product was located despite targeted searches (Jubo Health appears to be a separate long-term-care software company, not a LINE consumer service).
- **GrandPad, Storyworth, Famileo, and Marco Polo do not publish audited conversion, retention, or churn figures** — only Life360 and Tinybeans are public-company filings; all other subscriber/revenue numbers above come from press, reviews, or company marketing and should be treated as directional.
- **Sensor Tower/data.ai figures were only available second-hand** via blog summaries rather than the primary dashboard, and are flagged lower-confidence accordingly.
- The web-search budget for this session was exhausted before an explicit Life360 churn-rate disclosure could be located and before a Korean "Carely" app could be checked — worth a short follow-up pass if precise churn or a Korean coordination-app price point is needed later.
