## 3. What families want vs what elders tolerate

- **Acceptance gradient (Swiss national survey, n=1,211):** wearables for fall detection 81%, ambient sensors 59%, cameras 37% (bathroom 29%). 72% oppose sharing data with insurers; 53% need technical support. ([PMC12948381](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12948381/))
- **Japanese solo/couple households:** cameras and microphones face "slightly strong resistance"; door, temperature, CO2, water-flow sensors do not. ([PMC10305305](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10305305/))
- **Triggers for willingness:** change in living arrangement, a health event, or watching a peer's emergency; elders want image-free sensors and to control who sees what. ([PMC12736577](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12736577/))
- **Families/caregivers:** People Power's NIA study claims caregiver anxiety/depression reductions "comparable to pharmaceuticals"; the product families buy is *reassurance*, not detection accuracy. Adult children on forums default to Ring/Nest and negotiate placement with the parent — the pattern is "install, then discover parent resents it". ([peoplepowerfamily.com](https://www.peoplepowerfamily.com), [AgingCare](https://www.agingcare.com/questions/what-do-you-say-to-your-parent-when-you-want-to-install-nest-cameras-464690.htm))
- **Professional users:** alert fatigue is the #2 barrier after "ineffective standalone"; ~80% of bed/chair-alarm callouts false; LTC staff "overwhelmed with information", alarms "don't resolve themselves"; benefits accrue to hospitals while care orgs pay → no reimbursement path. ([PMC12688438](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12688438/), [Vayyar alert-fatigue blog](https://vayyar.com/blog/elderly-care/alert-fatigue))
- **Recurring B2C complaints across brands:** continued billing after cancellation (Aloe, Kami), subscription lock-in to a platform that then dies (Vayyar→Alexa Together; Sen.se Mother data lost), Wi-Fi dependency with no outage alert (Hex, Vayyar), placement/setup difficulty, false alarms on pass-by.

## 5. What is winning (2024–26)

1. **B2B facilities with image-abstracted optical or radar** and integration into nurse-call/EHR: Inspiren ($155M), Nobi (€35M), Teton ($20M), Vayyar via integrators, Butlr. Buyers pay because falls → liability, staffing, occupancy.
2. **Audio + agent software sold to home-care agencies**: Sensi ($98M) — the only "ambient" company with strong revenue growth, and it monetises caregiver QA, not elder safety per se.
3. **Prediction without hardware** (Cera) — NHS pays for outcomes, not sensors.
4. **Zero-cost utility/logistics bundles** (Japan; Howz via councils) for the mass low-acuity tier.

**Losing:** standalone B2C hardware + $40–100/mo subscriptions for PIR kits (dozens of near-identical brands, none with scale); B2C radar; B2C camera fall detection; Wi-Fi sensing as a consumer SKU.

## 6. Gaps

- **No B2C product combines** (a) true fall detection without a wearable, (b) elder-acceptable privacy (no images/mics), (c) <$30/mo, (d) no Wi-Fi dependency. Radar meets (a)(b) but fails (c); PIR kits meet (b)(c) but fail (a); Japanese meter services meet (c)(d) only.
- **No credible LLM-native consumer product**: LLM/agent work is in B2B agency layers (Sensi, Sagecare) and academia; nobody has shipped a family-facing agent that turns sensor streams into conversational, low-alert-fatigue summaries with escalation.
- **Distribution, not sensing, is the unsolved problem**: the survivors ride utilities, ISPs, councils, delivery fleets or facility operators. A family-paid subscription with no channel partner has a poor track record.
- **Alert design**: no vendor publishes precision/recall; the NAD action against Kami suggests accuracy claims are becoming a liability. Weekly "insight" reports (Envoy, Howz) rather than real-time alerts are the emerging middle ground.
- **Multi-occupant and platform-death risk** (Alexa Together, Mother) remain unaddressed in B2C offerings; cellular hubs (Canary, Just Checking, Aloe) are a differentiator few US B2C players have.
