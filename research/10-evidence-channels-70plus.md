# Evidence: Can We Reach and Be Answered by 70+ Parents, No Install? (Sept 2026)

## 1. Answer in five lines

1. **Russia**: partially, on a moving target. WhatsApp is still the 65+ default (57% of 65+ are WhatsApp-only, Mediascope 2025 via [Forbes.ru](https://www.forbes.ru/tekhnologii/556595-i-vtorye-stanut-pervymi-telegram-obosel-whatsapp-po-popularnosti-v-rossii)), while Telegram overtook WhatsApp in total reach in Jan 2026 ([Habr/Mediascope](https://habr.com/ru/news/1006878/)) and both are being throttled by the state. State messenger **MAX** is pre-installed by law since 1 Sept 2025 and had ~100M MAU by Apr 2026 ([Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/technology/articles/2026/04/09/1189129-max-stal-vtorim), [ixbt](https://www.ixbt.com/news/2026/04/27/max-max-100.html)) but has no published senior-adoption number and stores chats unencrypted under the Yarovaya law ([Такие дела](https://takiedela.ru/notes/max-v-plenu-u-fsb/)).
2. **Taiwan**: yes for the reachable half. LINE is near-universal (99.4% of 16+, NCC 2024) and 70+ internet use rose to 53.8% in 2025 ([TWNIC](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20251217701551-431202)) — meaning roughly **half of 70+ Taiwanese are still offline entirely**, so LINE-first reaches the digitally-active half, not the frailest quartile that most needs the product.
3. **Comparators**: WhatsApp (Germany/UK/India/Philippines), LINE (Japan) and KakaoTalk (Korea) each dominate for *connected* seniors, but every market shows a steep cliff at 65+ — UK 65+ spend well under half the daily time online of younger cohorts despite 92% population WhatsApp reach ([Ofcom 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/online-research/online-nation/2025/online-nations-report-2025.pdf?v=409837)); Korea's 65+ are only ~7% of KakaoTalk's user base despite ~97% national market share ([Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/984645/south-korea-kakaotalk-usage-by-age)).
4. **Ability**: a 70+ parent reliably *taps* one button or answers a known contact, but *initiating* (a voice note, a bot menu) is weaker — only 19% of Russians 60+ send voice messages and 26% report no technical ability to at all ([VCIOM 2023](https://wciom.ru/analytical-reviews/analiticheskii-obzor/golosovye-soobshchenija-pochemu-by-i-net)); a Taiwan LINE-bot check-in study got a 93.4% response rate on simple tasks but weaker engagement on photo-based flows ([Frontiers in Public Health 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13110929/)).
5. **Platform economics**: none of the five channels is a free, unlimited daily line at scale — WhatsApp utility templates cost $0.004–0.046/message outside a 24h window (Meta 2026), LINE's free tier caps at 200 pushes/month per account, MAX bots are restricted to verified Russian legal entities, and Viber charges ~€100–115/month flat plus per message — a daily check-in needs a budgeted paid tier, not the free window alone.

## 4. What it means for Vela

**Russia — don't build on one adapter.** WhatsApp is still where 65+ live (57% WhatsApp-only), so it stays primary for the pilot, but it's under state pressure (VoIP already blocked, domain instability since Feb 2026) and its pricing changes again 1 Oct 2026. Telegram suits the adult child but isn't where the parent already is. MAX can't be ignored — pre-installed by law, now larger than WhatsApp — but has no published senior-adoption evidence, its bot platform excludes individuals/informal teams (verified legal entity only), and it stores chats in plaintext with state access under Yarovaya, a real concern for a "peace of mind" product touching health topics. **Recommendation: pilot on WhatsApp with a Telegram fallback for the child's side; track MAX senior adoption before investing engineering there; never claim privacy guarantees MAX's architecture can't support.**

**Taiwan — LINE-first is right, but plan for the offline half.** LINE's near-universal reach (99.4%) and validated use in elder check-in bots (93.4% response rate) make it the obvious single adapter. But TWNIC's own 53.8% figure for 70+ internet use means roughly half of Taiwan's 70+ population is unreachable by any app — for that half, the parent surface must degrade to a phone call, not a simpler LINE screen. LINE's free tier (200 pushes/month) is far too small for daily check-ins per family; budget the paid tier from day one.

**What the parent surface must handle, everywhere.** Tapping a known contact's message or one big button is well-evidenced at 70+; *sending* a voice note is weaker and less certain — only 19% of 60+ Russians already do it, over a quarter report no technical ability. The reply loop should default to the lowest-effort input (tap / one word / missed-call-style signal), treating voice replies as a bonus, not the primary expected action — consistent with "I'm fine as fallback" already in the interaction model. The UTAUT chatbot study confirms this: effort expectancy and perceived physical condition significantly predict continued bot use, so every extra tap is a real drop-off risk.

**What fails.** (1) Any flow requiring the parent to cold-start a chat with a bot (no prior human thread) underperforms — trust comes from an existing contact, not a new "official account." (2) Assuming uniform smartphone possession fails for ~20% of older Russians (feature-phone users, disproportionately 65+) and roughly half of Taiwan's 70+ — these need a call/SMS fallback, not a messenger adapter. (3) Assuming free unlimited daily bot messages breaks budget — WhatsApp utility pricing, LINE's 200-message ceiling, and Viber's flat bot fee all impose real cost that must be priced into the paid "Light" tier.

## 5. Gaps

- No primary, age-broken-out data for **MAX adoption among 60+/65+/70+** — every figure found is all-ages (12+); the single biggest unknown for the Russia bet given MAX's mandatory status.
- Rosstat's most recent official 65-74 internet-use figure verified in this session is from **2021 (52.9%)**; a "~65%" figure for 2024/25 appears only in secondary text (a certificate error blocked one direct primary-source fetch).
- No figure found for **Telegram- or MAX-specific reach among 65+** specifically (only the WhatsApp 65+ split and all-ages MAX/Telegram totals) — the three-way RU comparison by age is incomplete.
- India, Philippines, and Germany figures rest on **secondary citations** (HelpAge India, DICT commentary, Bitkom/Statista aggregator text) rather than primary tables fetched directly — treat as directional.
- No evidence found on **hearing-related barriers to listening to voice messages** (vs. sending them) among 70+, despite being in scope — a real gap given Vela's dependence on the parent hearing prompts.
- LINE Messaging API's **Taiwan-specific (TWD) pricing** was not independently confirmed — figures came from USD/JPY-equivalent aggregator blogs, not LINE's own localized page.
- The session's **web search budget was exhausted** before India rural/feature-phone data and a primary HelpAge India report fetch could be completed — flagged rather than invented.
